## What One Million Coins Actually Is

One Million Coins drops you into an underground vault packed with exactly 1,000,000 gold pieces — and nothing but your bare hands to move them. The real appeal of One Million Coins coin collecting isn't the size of the pile; it's watching a slow, manual chore gradually turn into an automated gold rush as you bank hauls and reinvest the profits.

If you want to empty the bunker without wasting hours, you need to understand how the core loop, the tools, and the skill tree fit together. That is exactly what this One Million Coins coin collecting guide is for.

It's a short, first-person incremental game from solo developer and publisher ShoXx, released on October 13, 2026. Unlike endless idle games, the vault here is finite and countable: there's a real finish line when the last coin leaves the bunker.

## The Core Coin Collecting Loop, Step by Step

Every session in One Million Coins follows the same five-beat rhythm, and every upgrade you buy exists to shorten one of those beats.

| Stage | What you do | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Collect | Scoop coins from the pile with your hands or a tool | Raw gold starts in the vault, not in your wallet |
| Carry | Haul the load to a deposit station | Capacity decides how much a single trip is worth |
| Bank | Convert the haul into spendable funds | Only banked gold can buy upgrades |
| Upgrade | Spend funds on tools, bags, and skill nodes | Each purchase speeds up the next trip |
| Expand | Restore bunker infrastructure and dig deeper | Opens new space and faster routes |

Understanding the loop matters because the challenge here isn't combat or reflexes — it's logistics. Coins spawn in the vault, not in your account, and the only way gold becomes useful is if you physically carry it to a deposit station and turn it into funds.

That single rule explains almost every decision you'll make. When trips feel too short, you need capacity. When scooping feels slow, you need better tools. When you're spending more time walking than collecting, you need movement and reach. Reading the loop as a pipeline — collect, carry, bank, upgrade, expand — keeps you from buying the wrong thing at the wrong time.

Over time, the loop compresses. A trip that took thirty seconds at the start becomes a near-instant wave of coins once your tools and skill nodes stack up. That acceleration *is* the reward structure of the entire game.

## Tools, Carry Capacity, and the 250+ Node Skill Tree

### From Bare Hands to a Gold-Collecting Machine

You begin scooping coins by hand, which is deliberately tedious. Pouches come next, then backpacks, then progressively more powerful tools that pull in larger loads per action. Carry capacity is its own upgrade track because hauling is half the work.

| Phase | Typical gear | What changes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Start | Bare hands | Tiny scoops, constant trips back and forth |
| Early | Pouches | More coins per trip, fewer returns |
| Mid | Backpacks and upgraded tools | Bigger hauls and faster collection |
| Late | High-powered tools | Huge waves of coins collected at once |
| Endgame | Fully upgraded operation | The vault becomes your personal gold rush |

The late game is where the fantasy lands. High-powered tools sweep up massive waves of coins, and the vault transforms from a tedious chore into a self-feeding machine. The pacing is built around reaching that tipping point.

### Choosing Where to Spend in the 250+ Node Skill Tree

The skill tree is the deepest system in One Million Coins, with more than 250 nodes spread across seven upgrade paths. Confirmed upgrade categories include movement, reach, collection speed, tool strength, capacity, and deposit rate — the exact stats that map onto the five-beat loop.

| Upgrade focus | What it improves | When to invest |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Movement | How quickly you cross the vault | Early — travel time is the hidden tax |
| Reach | How far you can grab coins | When the pile spreads out |
| Collection speed | Coins per second per action | Constantly; it's never wasted |
| Tool strength | Power of your current equipment | Before jumping to a new tool tier |
| Capacity | How much you can carry per trip | Whenever trips feel too short |
| Deposit rate | How fast hauls convert to funds | When banking becomes the bottleneck |

Because the game is short, you can't comfortably max everything. Treat the tree as a set of answers to whatever is currently slowing you down. If you're constantly running back and forth, movement and capacity pay off. If you're standing still scooping forever, collection speed and tool strength win.

One quality-of-life note: you can save at any time, and the game is explicitly designed to be playable at your own pace, so there's no pressure to finish the tree in one sitting.

## Restoring the Bunker and Digging Deeper

Progression in One Million Coins isn't only about your gear — it's also about the vault itself. As you bank funds, you can repair and restore the bunker's infrastructure, which changes how efficiently gold moves from the pile to your account.

| Infrastructure | Role in the operation |
| --- | --- |
| Deposit stations | Convert hauled coins into spendable funds |
| Cargo lifts | Move gold and materials vertically through the bunker |
| Conveyors | Automate coin movement across long distances |
| Bunker systems | Restore deeper sections and open new excavation space |

Infrastructure upgrades are the closest thing the game has to automation. Cargo lifts and conveyors mean coins travel without you, and restoring deeper sections of the bunker unlocks new excavation space rather than just more of the same pile.

Digging deeper is also a pacing lever. You can push into new areas at your own pace, and doing so in bursts tends to beat grinding the same spot — new infrastructure usually outpaces the marginal gain from one more capacity node.

## Tips to Empty the Vault Faster

| Tip | Why it works |
| --- | --- |
| Bank every haul immediately | Unbanked gold is dead weight; funds only compound once spent |
| Balance capacity against speed | A huge backpack you can't fill is a wasted upgrade |
| Upgrade movement before raw power | Travel time is the biggest silent time sink early on |
| Reinvest instead of saving | Idle funds do nothing for your collection rate |
| Push deeper in bursts | New infrastructure beats grinding the same pile |
| Watch the seven paths, not one | A balanced build clears the vault faster than a lopsided one |

A few broader habits matter too. Bank early and often: every deposit you convert into tools starts paying off on the very next trip. Don't over-invest in capacity before you have the collection speed to fill it. And keep the finish line in mind — the vault holds exactly 1,000,000 coins, so every scoop is permanent, measurable progress toward the end.

## FAQ

### How long does it take to collect all one million coins?

The developer positions One Million Coins as a short, focused experience, and most players should be able to work through the vault in around two hours. Your actual time depends on how efficiently you spend on tools and skill nodes — a focused build that keeps the loop moving will finish noticeably faster than one that spreads points thin.

### Is One Million Coins coin collecting difficult?

Not in a reflexes sense. There are no quick-time events, you can save anytime, and the game is designed to be played at your own pace. The "difficulty" is optimization: deciding which upgrade removes your current bottleneck rather than simply buying whatever looks strongest.

### Can I play it with a controller?

Yes — Xbox controllers are supported, and the game is single-player with Steam Cloud saves, achievements, and leaderboards. Steam Deck compatibility was listed as unknown at launch, so verify that before planning a handheld run.

### Do I need a powerful PC to run it?

No. Minimum specs call for something like an Intel Core i5-8400 or AMD Ryzen 5 2600, 8 GB of RAM, and a GTX 1060 or RX 580, with 2 GB of storage. Recommended specs step up to an i7-9700K or Ryzen 5 3600 with 16 GB of RAM and an RTX 2060 or RX 6600. For a first-person incremental game, that's a modest footprint — most modern gaming PCs will handle One Million Coins without issue.
