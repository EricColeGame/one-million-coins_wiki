## Why Coin Farming Efficiency Decides Your Whole Run

One Million Coins is a short, first-person incremental game from solo developer ShoXx, released on October 13, 2026. You spawn with empty pockets and bare hands inside a bunker vault that contains exactly 1,000,000 coins. The premise never changes: scoop gold, carry it to a deposit station, bank it, and spend the proceeds on better equipment.

Because the vault is finite, your One Million Coins coin farming methods decide whether you clear it in a tight two-hour run or grind away for an entire evening. The game's own pacing target sits around two hours for most players — and the strongest One Million Coins coin farming methods all follow one rule: fix whichever step of the loop is slowest right now.

That loop only has four steps, which makes it easy to diagnose.

| Step | What you do | What it produces | Which upgrades it feeds |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Collect | Pull coins from the pile by hand or with tools | Loose gold in your carry space | Tool strength, collection speed |
| 2. Carry | Walk the haul to a deposit station | A pending deposit | Capacity, movement speed |
| 3. Bank | Convert the haul into spendable funds | Currency for the shop and skill tree | Deposit rate |
| 4. Upgrade | Buy tools, bags, and skill nodes | A faster, bigger next haul | All of the above |

Once you treat those four steps as separate problems, farming stops being about grinding harder and becomes about removing bottlenecks.

## The Core One Million Coins Coin Farming Methods, Phase by Phase

The vault is designed to be dug in layers, and each layer rewards a different approach. Using late-game tactics at the start wastes time; using early-game habits at the end wastes even more.

| Phase | Best farming method | Why it works | Common trap |
|---|---|---|---|
| Opening (bare hands) | Short, frequent trips to the closest deposit station | Small banked hauls compound into your first upgrades fast | Overloading and walking at a crawl |
| Early upgrades | Pouch first, then capacity nodes | Longer trips start paying off immediately | Buying speed before you can carry anything |
| Mid game | Backpacks plus restored deposit stations and conveyors | Travel time per coin drops sharply | Walking past infrastructure you could restore |
| Late game | High-powered tools that sweep up waves of coins | Bulk clearing outpaces anything manual | Letting collection outrun your deposit rate |

### Early Game: Short Trips Beat Big Hauls

With bare hands, your capacity is tiny and your movement is slow. The instinct is to grab everything you can touch — resist it. Three quick round trips will almost always bank more than one agonizing slow walk.

Spend your first funds on a pouch so each trip carries meaningfully more, then put a few nodes into movement so the walk itself stops being the bottleneck.

### Mid Game: Bags, Backpacks, and Fewer Trips

Once you own a backpack and have invested in capacity, your trip count drops and your average haul grows. This is the point where farming flips from "short and frequent" to "full and efficient."

It is also where restored infrastructure starts mattering more than raw tool power, because a shorter walk multiplies every future haul automatically.

### Late Game: Tools That Pull Waves of Coins

Late-game tools do not just collect faster — they collect in volume. Instead of scraping a pile, you pull in huge waves of coins at once. At that stage your bottleneck shifts almost entirely to deposit rate and travel, so keep upgrading those even when your tools feel unstoppable.

## Skill Tree Priorities That Multiply Your One Million Coins Coin Farming Methods

No list of One Million Coins coin farming methods is complete without the skill tree. It spans more than 250 nodes across seven upgrade paths, and the store page names movement, reach, collection speed, tool strength, capacity, and deposit rate as the attributes it improves.

That is a lot of choices, so prioritize by what is actually slowing you down.

| Upgrade path | What it changes | Priority | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Capacity | How much you carry per trip | High early, medium late | Diminishing returns once trips are long |
| Movement | How fast you travel the vault | High at every stage | The most underrated path in the tree |
| Reach | How far you can grab coins | Medium | Great for awkward pile shapes |
| Collection speed | How fast coins leave the pile | High mid-game | Pairs with tool strength |
| Tool strength | How much each pull yields | High late-game | Enables wave-style clearing |
| Deposit rate | How fast banked gold becomes funds | Medium to high | Becomes the real bottleneck late |

The practical takeaway: capacity solves the early game, movement solves the mid game, and deposit rate solves the late game. Tool strength and collection speed sit in the middle, amplifying whatever else you have built.

## Route Planning, Haul Logistics, and Bunker Infrastructure

Good One Million Coins coin farming methods are as much about geography as they are about gear. The vault is a physical space, and where you dig determines how far you carry.

### Bank Every Haul, Never Hold Gold

Coins sitting in your backpack are not funding your next upgrade. Gold only becomes spendable once it reaches a deposit station. If a run is more than half full and a station is nearby, bank it — the compounding from an earlier upgrade usually beats the extra coins you were carrying.

### Restore the Infrastructure You Walk Past

The bunker is not just scenery. Restoring it changes the shape of your farming routes.

| Infrastructure | What it restores | Effect on coin farming |
|---|---|---|
| Deposit stations | Banking points around the vault | Shortens the carry leg of every trip |
| Cargo lifts | Vertical transport between floors | Reconnects deeper sections to your banking route |
| Conveyors | Automated movement of gold | Adds throughput while you keep digging |
| Bunker systems | General vault function | Opens access to deeper, richer areas |

### Match the Tool to the Pile

Do not keep using a slow tool on a big pile just because it still works. As soon as a new tool unlocks and you can afford it, test it on the same pile you were just working. If the haul time drops, the upgrade paid for itself within a couple of trips.

## Mistakes That Waste Your Vault Time

Most slow runs come down to a handful of repeatable errors. Community reports and player experience point to the same short list again and again.

| Mistake | What it costs you | The fix |
|---|---|---|
| Maxing capacity with no movement upgrades | Long, slow, overloaded walks | Balance capacity with movement nodes |
| Hoarding unbanked gold | Upgrades you could already afford | Bank whenever a trip is half full |
| Ignoring reach upgrades | Wasted repositioning around piles | Take reach nodes when movement is capped |
| Skipping infrastructure restoration | Permanently longer routes | Restore stations, lifts, and conveyors early |
| Over-investing in one path | A build that stalls in the next phase | Spread points across the six core attributes |

A few habits keep you out of those traps:

- Re-evaluate your build every time you bank a large haul.
- Test a new tool on the pile you were already working before moving on.
- Restore any infrastructure you walk past twice.
- Keep movement upgrades flowing even when a flashier tool is available.
- Remember the goal is emptying the bunker completely, not collecting fast in one spot.

## FAQ

**How long does it take to collect all one million coins?**
The developer describes One Million Coins as a short, focused experience and estimates most players will work through the vault in around two hours. Efficient One Million Coins coin farming methods can push that number down, while unfocused grinding stretches it out considerably.

**What is the best early-game coin farming method in One Million Coins?**
Short, frequent trips. With bare hands and no bag, your capacity is the limiting factor, so banking small hauls quickly beats one giant slow walk. Buy a pouch as soon as you can afford it, then start adding movement nodes.

**Should I upgrade capacity or movement first?**
Capacity first, but not by much. Capacity makes each trip worth more; movement makes each trip cheaper in time. Once your hauls take more than a few seconds to deliver, movement becomes the better investment.

**Do conveyors and cargo lifts collect coins for me?**
No. Conveyors and lifts move gold and shorten your routes rather than mining for you. They are force multipliers — restoring them makes every manual haul you perform afterward faster.

**Is there a way to speed up the deposit station itself?**
Yes. Deposit rate is one of the attributes the skill tree improves, and it becomes the real bottleneck in the late game when high-powered tools are pulling in waves of coins faster than you can bank them.
