## Why a One Million Coins Speedrun Breaks the Usual Speedrun Rules

One million coins is the loudest number in *New Super Mario Bros. 2*, and it is also the most misunderstood. A **One Million Coins speedrun** is not a race to the flagpole — it is a race against a counter, and the counter only moves as fast as your routing allows. That distinction changes everything about how you plan an attempt.

Most Mario speedruns reward frame-perfect movement through a fixed set of levels. This one rewards resource management across dozens of short, randomized gauntlets. If you want to understand why the run takes the shape it does, you have to start with the coin economy — because playing the game normally will never get you to seven figures in a reasonable amount of time.

That is the whole reason the **One Million Coins speedrun** exists as a category at all. Nintendo built a goal that traditional play cannot realistically reach, then handed players a separate mode that can. Understanding that mode is the difference between a grind that takes years and a route that actually finishes.

## The Coin Economy: Where a Million Coins Actually Come From

Coin Rush is the engine of the entire category. In this mode you play three randomly selected courses back to back with a single life, competing against records collected from other players. A death ends the attempt before your total is banked, which is why consistency matters more than raw greed in a long run.

The critical detail is how rival records pay out. The first time you complete someone's tag, you receive coins equal to their recorded score — regardless of whether you beat it. A rival who banked 22,500 coins on the Mushroom Pack hands you 22,500 coins for simply finishing the attempt, plus whatever you collected yourself. That single mechanic is what makes a **One Million Coins speedrun** feasible.

Compare the payout rates side by side and the strategy becomes obvious.

| Coin source | Typical yield | Effort per coin | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Standard level play | Around 500 coins per level at best | Very high | Requires replaying levels repeatedly; slowest path |
| Coin Rush personal collection | Varies by course layout | Medium | Limited by the three randomly drawn courses |
| First clear of a rival's tag | Equal to that rival's record (e.g., 22,500) | Low | One-time payout per tag, and the biggest single source |
| Beating a rival's record | +1,000 coins (crown coin) | Medium | Repeatable on tags you have saved |
| Death during a Coin Rush run | 0 | Wasted | Ends the attempt before the total is banked |

The math is unforgiving. If a strong level nets you 500 coins, reaching one million through normal play means roughly two thousand perfect level clears. Route through tags with inflated records instead, and the same total becomes a manageable number of sessions. That is the entire thesis of the category in one sentence.

## Building a One Million Coins Speedrun Route, Step by Step

A route here is less about movement and more about scheduling. You are farming tags, protecting the good ones, and converting them into payouts in the right order. Here is the loop that most players converge on.

| Step | Action | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Finish the main game to unlock Coin Rush | The mode is the only realistic coin engine |
| 2 | Carry your console and collect tags | Tags are the raw material of the run |
| 3 | Play every new tag once, start to finish | The first clear pays out the rival's full record |
| 4 | Mark high-value tags as favorites | Favorites survive when new tags arrive |
| 5 | Re-run saved tags to hunt crown coins | Each beaten record adds another 1,000 coins |
| 6 | Repeat until the counter crosses 1,000,000 | The finish line is a number, not a flag |

The favorites feature deserves special attention. The tag list caps out, so a record you have not beaten yet can be pushed off the screen by newer arrivals. Favoriting a tag locks it in place, which means a brutal 22,500-coin record stays available for a later attempt instead of vanishing. In a **One Million Coins speedrun**, losing a good tag is a bigger setback than dying on a course.

### Managing the Tag List

The in-game list holds fifteen tags at once — enough to fill the entire Coin Rush screen. Community reports suggest that excess tags are discarded once the list is full, which makes curation part of the run itself. Treat your list like a deck: keep the highest-value records, cycle out anything you have already drained for its first-clear payout.

### Banking the First Clear

Resist the urge to restart a course because you fumbled a x2 flagpole touch. On a first clear, the rival's total is yours no matter how badly you play. Save the perfectionism for repeat attempts, when the crown coin is the only thing on the line.

## Coin Rush Optimization: Multipliers, Suits, and Flagpole Doubles

Once the tag economy is understood, the second layer of a **One Million Coins speedrun** is squeezing more coins out of each individual course. The game quietly signals how a rival earned their score — an icon on the results screen indicates whether the run was performed with the white raccoon suit, which is a strong hint about the route they used.

| Bonus or tool | Effect | Best used for |
|---|---|---|
| Gold Flower | Converts enemies into coins on contact | Dense enemy courses with heavy spawns |
| White raccoon suit | Improves coin collection and movement | Long stretches where survival is the risk |
| x2 flagpole touch | Doubles the end-of-course coin payout | Any course where you can reach the top cleanly |
| Crown coin | +1,000 coins per beaten rival record | Saved tags you can reliably outscore |
| Single-life discipline | Preserves the entire run total | Every attempt, without exception |

The flagpole double is the most underrated of these. It costs a few seconds and multiplies the coins you earned across three courses, which compounds fast over a long session. A run that consistently doubles its flagpole payouts will out-earn a faster run that skips them.

Survival is the other half of optimization. Because Coin Rush gives you one life, a greedy route through a dense enemy course can wipe out ten minutes of work in a single mistake. The best **One Million Coins speedrun** players pick courses they can finish every time, not courses with the theoretical maximum.

## Mistakes That Stall a One Million Coins Speedrun

Most failed attempts die from the same handful of errors. None of them are about reflexes.

| Mistake | Consequence | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Grinding standard levels for coins | Progress measured in weeks, not sessions | Move all coin farming into Coin Rush |
| Restarting a first-clear attempt | Throws away a guaranteed payout | Finish every first attempt, however sloppy |
| Never favoriting strong tags | High-value records get pushed off the list | Favorite anything above your average |
| Chasing crown coins too early | Deaths cost more than the 1,000-coin reward | Bank first clears before hunting records |
| Ignoring the flagpole double | Multiplied losses across every course | Practice the top-of-pole touch until it is automatic |
| Playing exhausted | One death erases an entire run | Stop while your clear rate is still high |

The first row is the one that ends most runs before they start. Traditional play feels productive because the counter moves, but a **One Million Coins speedrun** is a routing problem, not an endurance test. Fix the route and the counter takes care of itself.

## Community Reports: What the Grind Really Feels Like

Player experience with this challenge tends to split into two phases. The early phase is about discovery — unwrapping tags one at a time and watching records you have never seen before appear on the results screen. The rival's total is hidden until you finish the course, which keeps early attempts tense and honest. Many players describe that reveal as the best part of the mode.

The later phase is pure optimization. Once your tag list is stocked with high-value records, the run becomes a repeatable loop: clear, favorite, re-run, bank a crown coin, move on. Community reports from players in tag-dense regions describe picking up new tags constantly, which keeps the list churning and the payouts flowing. Players in quieter areas lean harder on their favorites.

The most common piece of advice from people who have actually finished is simple: enjoy the journey, because the destination is a number. The counter hits 1,000,000 and then it is over. What remains is the routing knowledge you built getting there — and that transfers directly to any future attempt.

## FAQ

**How long does a One Million Coins speedrun actually take?**

It depends almost entirely on how many high-value tags you can collect. Players with access to busy StreetPass areas report dramatically faster progress than players relying on a small pool of favorites. The route matters more than the hours logged.

**Can you reach one million coins without Coin Rush?**

Technically yes, but it is not practical. Standard levels yield a few hundred coins each at best, so a **One Million Coins speedrun** built on normal play would require thousands of level clears. Coin Rush and StreetPass tags are what make the goal realistic.

**What is the fastest way to gain coins in Coin Rush?**

Finish every new tag once for the guaranteed first-clear payout, favorite the records worth beating, and then re-run those favorites for crown coins. Layer in Gold Flower pickups and x2 flagpole touches to raise the ceiling on each attempt.

**Does dying in Coin Rush ruin a One Million Coins speedrun?**

It does not end the overall run, but it ends that attempt. Because you only get one life per gauntlet, a death means the coins from that set of three courses never reach your total. Consistent, safe routing beats aggressive routing over a long session.

**What should I do with tags I have already beaten?**

Once a tag has paid out its first-clear bonus and its crown coin, its value drops. Cycle it out of your favorites to make room for fresh records — but keep anything with a record you still cannot beat, since those are your best long-term sources of crown coins.
