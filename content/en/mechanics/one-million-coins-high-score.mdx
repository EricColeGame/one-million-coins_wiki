Chasing a **One Million Coins high score** turns a cozy coin-scooping sim into a race against your own worst habits. The vault holds exactly 1,000,000 coins, the game is designed to be finished in roughly two hours, and Steam leaderboards are supported — which means every inefficiency eventually shows up somewhere on a ranking. ## What Counts as a High Score in One Million Coins

The vault is finite. According to the official Steam store page for One Million Coins, you start with empty pockets and bare hands inside a bunker containing exactly one million coins, and the developer describes the experience as short and focused — most players should be able to work through it in around two hours. Because the finish line is fixed rather than endless, a score is not about grinding forever. It is about how efficiently you convert a fixed pile of gold into banked funds.

Steam Leaderboards appear in the game's feature list, so runs are tracked against other players. The store page does not publish the exact scoring formula, which is why the smartest approach is to optimize the variables every incremental game rewards: coins banked per minute, total elapsed time, and how completely you emptied the bunker.

Here are three benchmarks worth tracking yourself, even if you never look at a leaderboard:

| Benchmark | What it reveals | Mindset |
|---|---|---|
| Time to fully empty the vault | Raw end-to-end efficiency | Lower is better |
| Coins banked per minute at the halfway point | Whether your build is snowballing | Should rise sharply after 30 minutes |
| Upgrades purchased before the 30-minute mark | Early-game decision quality | More, cheaper upgrades first |

If all three numbers improve, your One Million Coins high score will improve with them.

## The Three Levers Behind Every Fast Run

Strip away the visuals and a fast run is governed by three numbers: how much you can carry, how fast you can collect, and how quickly you can convert a full load into spendable cash. Everything in the skill tree, every tool, and every piece of restored infrastructure feeds one of those three levers.

| Lever | What it controls | Where it comes from | Priority |
|---|---|---|---|
| Carry capacity | Coins per trip | Pouches, backpacks, capacity nodes | High early |
| Collection rate | Coins per second at the pile | Tool strength, collection speed nodes | High mid |
| Throughput | Banked coins per minute | Deposit rate nodes, cargo lifts, conveyors | Medium early, high late |
| Movement and reach | Seconds lost between pile and station | Movement and reach nodes | Medium |
| Infrastructure | Passive and automated hauling | Restored bunker systems | High late |

The trap most new players fall into is over-investing in collection speed before they have anywhere to put the coins. Collecting faster while carrying almost nothing just means you spend more time walking. Player experience with incremental games consistently shows the same pattern: capacity first, speed second, automation third.

## Skill Tree Priorities: Where to Spend Early Coins

One Million Coins ships with a 250+ node skill tree spread across seven upgrade paths. The store description names movement, reach, collection speed, tool strength, capacity, and deposit rate among the improvements available, plus deeper bunker infrastructure unlocked as you excavate.

### The seven paths at a glance

| Upgrade path | What it does | When to invest | Skip until |
|---|---|---|---|
| Capacity | Raises how many coins you carry per trip | Immediately | Never |
| Collection speed | Coins scooped per second | After first capacity tier | — |
| Tool strength | Makes each tool hit harder | Mid-game | After capacity |
| Movement | Faster travel around the bunker | Mid-game | After one capacity tier |
| Reach | Collect from farther away | Situational | Late |
| Deposit rate | Faster banking at the station | Once hauls get large | Early game |
| Infrastructure | Lifts, conveyors, restored stations | Late game | Until tools are strong |

### A simple spending order that works

1. **Buy the cheapest capacity node available.** Repeat until your first tool upgrade is affordable.
2. **Grab your first real tool.** Manual scooping has the worst coins-per-second rate in the game.
3. **Alternate capacity and collection speed.** Keep the ratio roughly even so you never walk with a half-full bag.
4. **Add movement once trips get long.** Time spent walking is time not spent collecting.
5. **Pivot to deposit rate and infrastructure** as soon as your hauls start overflowing the station's throughput.

This order keeps your income curve steep. The single biggest score killer is a flat income curve in the middle of a run.

## A Phase-by-Phase Route to a Faster Clear

Think of a run as five phases rather than one continuous grind. Each phase has a clear exit condition — once you hit it, stop optimizing the current setup and move on.

| Phase | Goal | Exit condition | Common stall |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Bare hands | Bank your first coins | First tool purchased | Scooping too long by hand |
| 2. First tools | Build a capacity base | Consistent multi-hundred-coin trips | Ignoring capacity nodes |
| 3. Expansion | Unlock bags and backpacks | Loads measured in the thousands | Buying power without storage |
| 4. Infrastructure | Restore lifts, conveyors, stations | Deposit rate stops being a bottleneck | Forgetting to restore the bunker |
| 5. Final sweep | Empty the vault completely | Zero coins remain | Leaving scattered piles behind |

The final sweep is where leaderboard runs are won and lost. Late-game tools are designed to pull in huge waves of coins, so resist the urge to hunt individual stragglers by hand. Work the vault from the deepest point outward and let the big tools do the heavy lifting.

### Mistakes that quietly ruin your run

| Mistake | Hidden cost | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Buying one expensive upgrade instead of several cheap ones | Slower compounding | Prioritize the cheapest useful node |
| Ignoring the bunker restoration systems | Manual hauling forever | Restore infrastructure as soon as it unlocks |
| Walking with a partially full bag | Wasted trips | Match capacity to collection rate |
| Skipping movement nodes | Minutes of dead time | Take movement once trips exceed a few seconds |
| Chasing scattered coins early | Inefficient sweeping | Save the cleanup for late-game tools |

None of these mistakes will end a run. Together, though, they are easily worth fifteen to thirty minutes — which is enormous in a two-hour game.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Does One Million Coins have a high score leaderboard?**

Yes. Steam Leaderboards are listed among the game's features, so runs are tracked against other players. The store page does not publish the exact scoring formula, so the practical approach is to optimize total completion time, coins banked per minute, and how completely you empty the vault — the three things any leaderboard in a finite incremental game has to care about.

**How long does a full run take?**

The developer describes One Million Coins as a short, focused experience that most players should work through in around two hours. A practiced run built around capacity and infrastructure can come in noticeably under that, which is exactly where a competitive One Million Coins high score comes from.

**What is the best first upgrade?**

Capacity. Buying carry space before collection speed keeps you from walking with a half-full bag, which is the single most common source of wasted time in the early game. Once capacity and collection rate are roughly balanced, move on to your first real tool.

**Do I need to collect every single coin for a good score?**

The vault is finite and the end goal is emptying the bunker completely, so yes — every coin counts. The trick is not skipping coins, but collecting the last of them with high-powered late-game tools instead of hunting stragglers by hand while your income curve flattens out.

**Is it worth replaying after the first clear?**

Absolutely. A second run is where route knowledge pays off. You already know where the deposit stations are, which infrastructure unlocks first, and how the skill tree branches, so you can cut the wasted early minutes that cost you the most time on your first attempt.
