The vault in One Million Coins holds exactly 1,000,000 coins, and that number only ever goes down. That single design choice is why the **One Million Coins bottom discovery** matters so much: it's the phase where the pile in front of you stops being the main obstacle and the distance to the deposit station takes over. Plan for that shift, and a clean run stays clean. Ignore it, and the final layer turns into a slow, repetitive grind.

Developer ShoXx built this as a short, focused first-person incremental experience, and the official Steam listing frames the finish line as emptying the bunker completely. So the One Million Coins bottom discovery isn't a hidden cutscene or a secret boss — it's the deepest layer of the vault, plus the moment your build has to carry the last stretch on its own.

## What the One Million Coins Bottom Discovery Actually Means

Because the vault is finite, the game has a real ending condition rather than an endless number that keeps climbing. Every scoop you carry to a deposit station permanently removes coins from the world. The bottom discovery is simply what happens when the easy surface coins are gone and you're working the lowest accessible layer of the pile.

That reframes your priorities. Early on, the limiting factor is how fast you can scoop. Later, it's how far you have to walk, how much you can carry, and how quickly a haul converts into spendable funds. The official Steam page for [One Million Coins](https://store.steampowered.com/app/5258340/One_Million_Coins/) confirms the intended structure: collect, bank, upgrade, dig deeper, and restore bunker infrastructure along the way.

At the time of writing, the Steam page shows only a couple of user reviews, so community consensus is still forming. Player experience so far leans positive, but treat any "best build" advice as early-days opinion rather than settled math.

### Why the last coins are the most expensive

Per-coin cost in an incremental mining game isn't the coin itself — it's the travel time attached to it. Near the surface, a handful of coins sits a few steps from the deposit station. Near the bottom, the same handful may require a much longer round trip. That's why capacity and movement upgrades tend to matter more at the end than raw scooping speed.

## The 250+ Node Skill Tree: Where to Spend Before the Bottom Discovery

The store description advertises a skill tree with more than 250 nodes spread across seven upgrade paths, and names six attributes specifically: movement, reach, collection speed, tool strength, capacity, and deposit rate. Those six cover the vast majority of what you'll feel during a run.

| Upgrade path | What it improves | Priority near the bottom |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Movement | Travel speed between the pile and the deposit station | High |
| Reach | How far you can grab coins without repositioning | High |
| Collection speed | How fast each scoop resolves | Medium-high |
| Tool strength | How much each tool pulls per action | High |
| Capacity | How much you can carry per trip | Very high |
| Deposit rate | How fast a haul converts into funds | Medium |

Because there are seven paths total and only six named attributes, expect at least one branch to be more situational than the rest. The practical takeaway is simple: **capacity and movement are the two stats that scale with depth.** A build with enormous scooping power and a small backpack will spend most of its bottom-layer time walking.

### A simple spending rule

Keep capacity roughly one upgrade ahead of your current layer, and movement roughly level with it. When you notice yourself making more trips than scoops, you've drifted too far into raw tool power. When you notice coins sitting just out of reach, you've underinvested in reach.

## A Practical Bottom Discovery Checklist

Use this sequence when you're preparing to push into the deepest part of the vault. It's built around the order in which problems actually appear, not the order upgrades appear in the menu.

1. **Bank before you dig.** Never start a deep push with a full unbanked haul. Deposit first so your funds are available the moment a useful upgrade unlocks.
2. **Cap your carry, then chase speed.** Capacity determines how much value each round trip is worth. Speed only multiplies what you're already carrying.
3. **Stay ahead on reach.** Reach determines whether coins are collectible from where you stand. Falling behind here forces extra repositioning on every scoop.
4. **Restore infrastructure as soon as it's offered.** Deposit stations, cargo lifts, and conveyors reduce the manual part of the loop, which is exactly what the bottom layer punishes.
5. **Do a final sweep pass.** Late-game tools pull in large waves of coins. Once you have them, walk the vault again rather than hunting individual stragglers by hand.

| Checklist item | Problem it prevents | When to do it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bank every haul | Wasted funds sitting idle | Before each deep push |
| Capacity upgrade | Too many round trips | Every layer transition |
| Reach upgrade | Coins stranded out of range | Whenever you reposition often |
| Infrastructure restoration | Manual hauling bottleneck | As soon as it unlocks |
| Full sweep with late tools | Missing coins at the end | Final 10% of the vault |

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is the One Million Coins bottom discovery a hidden area or secret?**

No. Based on the developer's own description, the bottom is the deepest layer of the underground vault, and the completion condition is emptying the bunker entirely. There's no separate secret zone described in the official material — the "discovery" is the experience of reaching the lowest layer and finishing the sweep.

**How long does it take to reach the bottom of the vault?**

The developer estimates roughly two hours for most players to work through the vault. Your actual time depends on how efficiently you spend skill points, especially in capacity and movement, since those two stats control how much time you spend walking rather than collecting.

**Do I need to max out the skill tree to finish the One Million Coins bottom discovery?**

No. With 250+ nodes across seven paths, a full unlock is not the same as a full clear. Target the attributes that match your current bottleneck — capacity, movement, reach, tool strength, collection speed, or deposit rate — and skip nodes that don't address it.

**Can I play the bottom layer at my own pace?**

Yes. The game is single-player and the store listing highlights accessibility options including save-anytime support and playable-at-your-own-pace design. There's no timer pressuring the run, so you can bank, upgrade, and dig at whatever speed suits you.

## The Bottom Line

The One Million Coins bottom discovery is less about a dramatic reveal and more about a shift in how you play. Bank every haul, keep your carry capacity ahead of your depth, restore the bunker infrastructure as it unlocks, and finish with a full sweep.
