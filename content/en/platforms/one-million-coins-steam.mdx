## The Core Loop: Scoop, Bank, Upgrade

The rhythm of One Million Coins is simple, and it stays simple on purpose. You grab coins from an enormous pile, haul them to a deposit station, convert them into spendable funds, and spend those funds on something that makes the next haul faster. Every upgrade feeds the next trip, which is the classic incremental dopamine ladder — just with a body attached to it.

What separates this from a spreadsheet with graphics is the physicality. Early on, your carry capacity is tiny, so you are walking a short loop over and over. By the late game, you are running wide loads through restored bunker infrastructure while high-powered tools vacuum up waves of gold. The store page describes the endgame as turning the vault into "your personal gold rush," and that escalation curve is the whole point.

| Loop Stage | What You Do | What It Unlocks |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Scoop by hand | Collect coins with your bare hands | Your first few dollars |
| 2. Bank the haul | Deposit gold at the station | Spendable funds |
| 3. Buy tools | Pouches, backpacks, better collectors | Larger loads, faster pickups |
| 4. Invest skill points | Spend across the 250+ node tree | Speed, reach, capacity, deposit rate |
| 5. Restore infrastructure | Bring back deposit stations, cargo lifts, conveyors | Automated, hands-free flow |
| 6. Empty the vault | Chase the final coins | Completion and achievements |

### Why the Finite Vault Matters

The single most important design decision here is that the vault contains a fixed 1,000,000 coins. Nothing regenerates. Nothing respawns. When the bunker is empty, the game is over, and that changes how you play. Instead of optimizing for an infinite grind, you are optimizing for a two-hour sprint. You can genuinely plan a route, budget your skill points, and finish a run in an evening.

That structure also makes the One Million Coins Steam experience unusually friendly to short sessions. Accessibility features listed on the store page include the ability to save anytime, play at your own pace, and skip quick-time events entirely — no reflex checks, no punishment for stepping away.

## Tools, Capacity, and the Skill Tree

Progression in One Million Coins runs on two tracks that constantly feed each other: equipment and skills. Equipment determines what you can physically carry and how fast you can collect. Skills determine how efficiently all of that works together.

| Upgrade Path | What It Improves | Why It Matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Movement | How fast you travel the bunker | Travel time is your biggest early cost |
| Reach | How far you can grab coins | Fewer repositioning steps per scoop |
| Collection speed | How fast coins leave the pile | Direct throughput gain |
| Tool strength | How much each tool pulls per action | Multiplies every other stat |
| Carry capacity | How much gold fits in your bags | Fewer round trips to the deposit station |
| Deposit rate | How quickly gold converts to funds | Shortens the loop tail |
| Infrastructure | Cargo lifts, conveyors, stations | Automates the busywork |

A few practical notes from the design itself: the store page confirms the tree spans 250+ nodes across seven upgrade paths, which means early points are cheap and plentiful. The trap most new players fall into is dumping everything into raw collection speed while ignoring capacity and movement. If you cannot carry the gold and cannot walk it back quickly, faster scooping does not help you.

## Tips for Emptying the Vault Faster

Early player experience since launch has been limited — only a couple of reviews are up so far — but the systems themselves make a few strategies obvious. Treat these as informed starting points rather than gospel, and adjust as you learn the bunker layout.

| Tip | Why It Works |
| --- | --- |
| Bank early and often | Deposit rate and capacity upgrades compound over dozens of trips |
| Buy capacity before speed | Fewer trips beats faster scooping in the first hour |
| Never skip movement nodes | Travel time dominates your early loop |
| Spend points on cheap nodes first | Low-tier nodes give the best return per point |
| Restore conveyors and lifts immediately | They remove manual hauling, your biggest time sink |
| Use save-anytime to your advantage | Short sessions still make progress |
| Plan upgrades before the pile runs dry | The final stretch is slow — do not get caught with no funds |

One more strategic note: because the vault is finite, the endgame is a race against diminishing returns. As the pile shrinks, your collection rate naturally drops even with top-tier tools. That is the moment to lean on infrastructure and deposit efficiency rather than raw tool power. Players who front-load throughput and back-load automation tend to finish cleanly; players who do the opposite spend their last thirty minutes walking in circles.

## FAQ

**Is One Million Coins Steam worth buying?**
For $1.99 during the introductory offer, yes — if you enjoy incremental progression and want a game with a real ending. Two hours of focused play for the price of a coffee is a fair trade, and the 16 achievements plus leaderboards add replay value. If you need long-form content, look elsewhere.

**Does One Million Coins work on Steam Deck?**
Compatibility is currently listed as Unknown on the store page. The system requirements are light enough that it seems likely to run, but there is no official verification yet, so check the store page before purchasing if handheld play matters to you.

**Does One Million Coins have multiplayer or co-op?**
No. It is a single-player experience, though Steam Family Sharing and Steam Cloud are both supported, so you can share it across a household and carry your save between machines.
