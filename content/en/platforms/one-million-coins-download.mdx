## How the Gameplay Loop Actually Works

Strip away the upgrade systems and One Million Coins is one very simple idea executed with a lot of momentum. You are dropped into an underground vault containing exactly 1,000,000 coins with empty pockets and nothing but your bare hands. Everything after that is acceleration.

| Stage | What you are doing | What it unlocks |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Manual scooping | Picking coins up by hand | Your first deposit |
| 2. Banking hauls | Carrying gold to the deposit station | Spendable funds |
| 3. Tool upgrades | Buying better collection equipment | Faster gathering |
| 4. Capacity upgrades | Pouches and backpacks | Bigger loads per trip |
| 5. Skill tree investment | Spending on movement, reach, speed, capacity | Compounding efficiency |
| 6. Infrastructure restoration | Rebuilding lifts, conveyors, deposit stations | Automated hauling |
| 7. Late-game tools | High-powered equipment that pulls in waves of coins | A full-scale gold rush |

The rhythm is the classic incremental hook: every haul is slightly faster than the last, and the gap between "this feels slow" and "I am a gold-collecting machine" closes faster than you expect. Because the vault is finite and the coin count is fixed, there is also a genuine finish line. You are not chasing an infinite number — you are working toward emptying the bunker completely.

For a first-person game, it is unusually hands-on. You are physically carrying loads rather than watching a progress bar, which is what separates the One Million Coins download from the browser-based incrementals it borrows from.

## Progression, Skill Tree, and Tips for a Faster Vault Clear

The depth here comes from a **250+ node skill tree spread across seven upgrade paths**. The store page names the broad areas it improves: movement, reach, collection speed, tool strength, carry capacity, and deposit rate.

| Upgrade Area | What It Improves | Why It Matters |
|---|---|---|
| Movement | How fast you traverse the vault | Cuts dead time between pile and deposit station |
| Reach | How far you can grab coins | Fewer repositioning trips |
| Collection speed | How quickly you scoop | Directly multiplies every other upgrade |
| Tool strength | How much each tool pulls per action | Scales your output as tools get stronger |
| Capacity | How much you can carry | Fewer round trips per haul |
| Deposit rate | How fast gold converts to funds | Removes the bottleneck at the station |

Because there are so many nodes, it is easy to spread yourself thin. A few habits help:

- **Bank before you shop.** Spending only happens from banked funds, so a completed deposit is always the priority over a partially filled bag.
- **Capacity first, speed second.** Early on, most of your lost time is walking back and forth. Bigger loads shrink that faster than faster scooping does.
- **Reinvest immediately.** Sitting on unspent funds does nothing for you. The loop rewards constant turnover.
- **Restore infrastructure when it becomes available.** Lifts and conveyors change the shape of a run more than a small stat bump will.
- **Play at your own pace.** The store explicitly lists the game as playable at your own pace and save-anytime, so there is no penalty for stepping away.

One more thing worth flagging for anyone planning a One Million Coins download: the game is listed with no quick-time events, custom volume controls, and stereo sound among its accessibility features. It is a low-pressure game by design.
