## One Million Coins System Requirements at a Glance

If you're eyeing ShoXx's bite-sized mining sim, the first box to tick is the One Million Coins system requirements — nothing flattens a two-hour gold rush faster than a slideshow on your desktop. The good news is that the One Million Coins system requirements are refreshingly modest for a 3D first-person game, and most gaming PCs built in the last five or six years should clear them without breaking a sweat. This guide breaks down every listed spec, explains what each one means in practice, and flags the platform quirks worth knowing before you spend your two dollars.

One Million Coins launched on October 13, 2026, and the store listing keeps things simple: one minimum sheet, one recommended sheet, and a 2 GB install. There is no ray tracing toggle, no upscaler requirement, and no SSD mandate. If your machine handles modern indie 3D games at 1080p, you are almost certainly fine.

## Minimum vs. Recommended: What Each Spec Really Means

Spec sheets are written for lawyers as much as players, so it helps to translate them. The minimum column describes the hardware needed to run the game at all at a playable frame rate, typically at 1080p with reduced settings. The recommended column describes the hardware that gives you headroom to spare.

The CPU gap between the two tiers is smaller than it looks. An Intel Core i5-8400 and an AMD Ryzen 5 2600 are both six-core chips from 2017–2018, and the recommended i7-9700K or Ryzen 5 3600 are simply faster versions of the same idea. Incremental games are usually light on the processor, so this is less about raw speed and more about avoiding a hard bottleneck.

The GPU jump matters more. A GTX 1060 6 GB and an RX 580 8 GB are the classic 1080p workhorses of the previous console generation. An RTX 2060 or RX 6600 roughly doubles the available rendering muscle. Here is a rough guide to how common cards stack up, based on general performance tiers rather than any official benchmark:

| Your graphics card | Relative tier | What to expect |
|---|---|---|
| GTX 1050 Ti / RX 570 | Below minimum | May boot, but expect stutter and low frame rates |
| GTX 1060 6 GB / RX 580 8 GB | Minimum | Playable 1080p, medium-ish settings |
| GTX 1660 Super / RX 5600 XT | Between tiers | Comfortable 1080p, high settings |
| RTX 2060 / RX 6600 | Recommended | Smooth 1080p with headroom for higher resolutions |
| RTX 3060 or better | Above recommended | No meaningful limitation |

Memory is the other clear dividing line: 8 GB for minimum, 16 GB for recommended. Because the game itself is tiny and runs in a single-player session, 8 GB will genuinely work if you are not leaving a browser with forty tabs open behind it.

## Do You Need the Recommended Specs to Enjoy It?

Short answer: no, and here is why the One Million Coins system requirements are more forgiving than they look on paper. One Million Coins is a short, focused experience that the developer expects most players to finish in roughly two hours. It is not an open-world game streaming textures from a sprawling map — it is a single vault, a finite pile of exactly 1,000,000 coins, and a compact bunker you gradually restore.

That design keeps the rendering load predictable. The heaviest moments come late in a run, when upgraded tools pull in huge waves of coins at once and the vault becomes a swirling mass of physics objects. That is the scenario where a below-minimum GPU will feel the pinch, not the early hand-scooping phase.

Community reports are still thin — the game had only a handful of user reviews shortly after launch, and all of them positive — so treat any performance claim from players as early-days anecdote rather than a benchmark. What is safe to say is that the hardware bar here sits well below what most current releases demand.

| Your setup | Verdict | Expected experience |
|---|---|---|
| Modern gaming laptop with a discrete GPU | Comfortably above minimum | Smooth, no tweaking needed |
| Desktop with GTX 1060 / RX 580 and 8 GB RAM | Meets minimum | Playable, especially at 1080p |

| Older quad-core laptop with 4 GB RAM | Below minimum | Not supported |

## How to Squeeze Out More Performance

If your PC lands somewhere between the two tiers, a few general adjustments will usually close the gap. None of these require special tools, and none of them change how the game plays.

- **Lower your output resolution first.** Dropping from 1080p to 1600x900 or 720p is the single biggest lever you have, and it costs almost nothing visually in a stylized, cartoon-shaded game.
- **Close background applications.** Browsers, launchers, and capture software all compete for the same 8 GB of RAM the minimum spec assumes you have.
- **Update your GPU drivers.** The minimum-spec cards here are several generations old, and current drivers often carry meaningful fixes for newer releases.
- **Install on an SSD if you can.** The install is only 2 GB, so the cost is trivial, and it removes one source of asset-streaming hitching.
- **Verify your game files** through Steam if you hit crashes on first launch. A partial download is a more common culprit than bad hardware.
- **Let the first minute settle.** Opening a fresh vault means compiling shaders and spawning a large pile of coin objects; brief stutter right after loading is normal on minimum-spec machines.

| Symptom | Likely cause | Practical fix |
|---|---|---|
| Stutter in the first minute | Initial asset and shader loading | Wait it out; install on SSD |
| Low frame rate during big coin waves | GPU below the minimum tier | Reduce resolution and settings |
| Crash immediately at launch | Outdated or missing GPU driver | Update drivers, then verify files |
| Game refuses to start | 32-bit Windows or unsupported OS | Requires 64-bit Windows 10 or 11 |
| Long load times | Mechanical hard drive | Move the 2 GB install to an SSD |

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What are the minimum One Million Coins system requirements?**
You need 64-bit Windows 10, an Intel Core i5-8400 or AMD Ryzen 5 2600, 8 GB of RAM, an NVIDIA GTX 1060 with 6 GB or an AMD RX 580 with 8 GB, DirectX 11, and 2 GB of free storage. The recommended tier steps up to a Core i7-9700K or Ryzen 5 3600, 16 GB of RAM, and an RTX 2060 or RX 6600.

**Can I run One Million Coins on a laptop?**
Yes, provided the laptop has a dedicated graphics chip. A gaming laptop with a discrete GPU will clear the One Million Coins system requirements easily. A thin-and-light machine relying on integrated graphics sits below the minimum and is unlikely to run the game acceptably.

**Is One Million Coins playable on Steam Deck?**
Valve had not issued a compatibility rating at launch, so the status is officially unknown. The game does list Xbox controller support, which is a good sign for gamepad play, but you should treat handheld performance as unverified until Valve publishes a verdict.

**Do I really need 16 GB of RAM?**
Not strictly. The 16 GB figure belongs to the recommended tier, not the minimum. With 8 GB you can play comfortably as long as you close other memory-hungry programs, especially since the entire game install is only 2 GB.

**Is there a Mac or Linux version?**
No native build was advertised at launch. The One Million Coins system requirements reference Windows 10 and 11 exclusively, so Mac and Linux players would need a compatibility layer or a Windows machine to join in.
