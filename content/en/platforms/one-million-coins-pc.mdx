## The Core Loop: Dig, Bank, Upgrade, Repeat

Every session of One Million Coins PC follows the same four-beat rhythm, and understanding that rhythm is the closest thing the game has to a skill ceiling.

| Stage | What You Do | Why It Matters |
|---|---|---|
| Dig | Break into coin-packed terrain with your current tool | Reveals coins and opens routes deeper into the vault |
| Collect | Sweep up loose coins as you go | Coins you're carrying aren't spendable yet |
| Bank | Deposit your haul at the bank | Banked coins become currency for upgrades |
| Upgrade | Spend coins on stronger tools and faster movement | Each purchase shortens the next run |
| Repeat | Head back in with better gear | Compounding speed is the real goal |

The trap for new players is treating this like a treasure hunt. It isn't. One Million Coins PC is a throughput game: the question is never "how many coins can I find?" but "how many coins can I bank per minute?"

That distinction changes how you play. Someone who banks early and often will out-earn someone who hoards a giant haul before walking back. Frequent deposits mean frequent upgrades, and frequent upgrades mean the vault starts dissolving under your tool. The compounding effect is the entire fantasy the game is selling.

## Upgrade Priorities and Common Mistakes

### What to Buy First

There's no single correct build order, but the underlying logic is consistent: buy whatever removes the biggest bottleneck in your current run.

| Priority | Upgrade Type | Why It Comes First |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Digging power / tool tier | Faster extraction from the very first second of a run |
| 2 | Movement speed | Less time walking between the vault face and the bank |
| 3 | Carry capacity | Fewer bank trips per run |
| 4 | Banking convenience | Removes a manual step from the loop entirely |
| 5 | Cosmetic or endgame extras | Fun, but they don't shorten your runs |

Treat this as a framework rather than a fixed list — exact upgrade names and costs depend on the build you're playing, and the store page is the best place to confirm what's currently in the game.

The universal rule: if you're spending more time walking than digging, buy speed. If you're spending more time banking than digging, buy capacity. If digging itself feels slow, buy power.

### Mistakes That Slow You Down

| Mistake | Why It Hurts | Better Approach |
|---|---|---|
| Hoarding coins before banking | Unbanked coins can't buy upgrades | Deposit every time you pass the bank |
| Buying everything evenly | Spreads coins thin across upgrades | Fix your current bottleneck first |
| Ignoring the coin counter | You lose track of how close the goal is | Check progress periodically |
| Playing in marathon sessions | Short loops reward frequent, focused runs | Play in shorter, purposeful bursts |
| Skipping patch notes | Balance changes can shift the best build | Read update notes before a long session |

One habit worth building: end every session on an upgrade. Stopping right after a purchase means your next session starts with a faster tool and an immediate sense of momentum. That's the psychological trick incremental games rely on, and One Million Coins PC uses it well.

Player experience with short incremental games points to the same conclusion — the people who finish fastest aren't the ones who play the longest, but the ones who keep the loop tight.
