## What the One Million Coins Official Trailer Actually Shows

Short, cheap, and weirdly hypnotic — that's the pitch of the One Million Coins official trailer, a 49-second gameplay clip published alongside the game's Steam launch on October 13, 2026. If you've ever lost an evening to an incremental game, this one poses a simple question: how fast can you empty a vault that contains exactly 1,000,000 coins? The One Million Coins official trailer answers it in under a minute, and the answer is "much faster than you'd expect, once you buy better tools."

That is the entire hook, and it's a smart one. There's no cinematic story setup, no narrator explaining a lore-drenched world, and no drawn-out teaser for content that may never ship. What you get instead is raw in-game footage of a first-person character scooping gold by hand, hauling it to a deposit station, and watching the numbers climb. It's the kind of trailer that tells you exactly what the game is in the time it takes to microwave a burrito.

## The Core Loop: Scoop, Bank, Upgrade, Repeat

Incremental games live or die on whether the repetition feels rewarding, and the One Million Coins official trailer makes the loop legible in seconds. You start with empty pockets and bare hands. You grab coins. You carry them to a deposit station. You convert them into spendable cash. You spend that cash on something that makes the next trip faster.

The developer, ShoXx, describes the finished game as a short, focused first-person incremental mining experience designed to be completed in roughly two hours. That's a deliberate choice: this isn't a live-service grind you're meant to play for months. It's a compact, finite vault with a visible finish line.

| Loop stage | What you do | What improves it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Collect | Scoop coins from the pile by hand or with tools | Tool strength, collection speed, reach |
| Carry | Haul your load to the deposit station | Pouches, backpacks, capacity upgrades |
| Bank | Convert gold into spendable funds | Deposit rate and deposit-station upgrades |
| Upgrade | Spend funds on tools and skill nodes | Skill tree investment across seven paths |
| Expand | Restore lifts, conveyors, and bunker infrastructure | Deeper excavation and faster automation |

The satisfying part, based on what the footage shows, is the compression of time. Early trips are slow and manual. Later trips, if the trailer's late-game glimpses are honest, turn the vault into a conveyor-fed gold rush where coins flow in waves rather than handfuls.

## Skill Tree, Tools, and Carry Capacity Explained

The most interesting mechanical detail the One Million Coins official trailer hints at is depth behind the simplicity. Underneath the scooping is a skill tree with more than 250 nodes spread across seven upgrade paths. That's a surprising amount of build variety for a game priced like a cup of coffee.

According to the developer's own description, those nodes affect movement speed, reach, collection speed, tool strength, carry capacity, and deposit rate. In practice, that means two players can approach the same vault with genuinely different strategies — one leaning into raw collection speed, another into huge carrying capacity so each trip banks a fortune.

| Upgrade path | What it changes | Who it suits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Movement | How quickly you cross the bunker | Players who hate backtracking |
| Reach | How far you can grab coins from | Fans of efficient, low-effort scooping |
| Collection speed | How fast each scoop fills your bag | Fast, twitchy upgrade rushers |
| Tool strength | How much a single swing or scoop yields | Big-number enthusiasts |
| Capacity | How much you can carry before depositing | Players who prefer fewer, fatter trips |
| Deposit rate | How fast gold converts into funds | Anyone optimizing the bank step |
| Infrastructure | Restoring lifts, conveyors, and stations | Players who want automation to do the work |

If you're the type who respecs three times before finishing a tutorial, this structure is the main reason to care. The One Million Coins official trailer doesn't spell out every node, but the UI glimpses suggest a system built for tinkering.

## One Million Coins Official Trailer vs. the Full Game: What Holds Up

Trailers are marketing, so it's fair to ask how much of the pitch survives contact with the actual build. Based on the store listing and early community reports, most of the core promises appear to be accurate — with a few caveats worth knowing before you buy.

| Trailer impression | What the full game actually offers |
| --- | --- |
| Fast, escalating progression | Yes, but the opening is deliberately slow and manual |
| Big late-game tools | Present, but you'll spend real time earning them |
| A huge vault to conquer | A finite 1,000,000-coin pile with a definite end |
| Endless replayability | No — it's a roughly 2-hour focused experience |
| Polished, cozy presentation | Stylized and cartoony, matching the footage |
| Deep upgrade system | 250+ skill nodes across seven upgrade paths |

The biggest mismatch is scope. Anyone expecting a hundred-hour idle game will be disappointed; anyone wanting a tight weekend project will be thrilled. Player experience reported on Steam at launch was limited — only two user reviews were listed, both positive, which isn't enough for the platform to generate an aggregate score yet. Treat that as a promising early signal rather than a verdict.

Other features confirmed on the store page include Steam Achievements (16 of them, with names like "Pocket Change" and "A Small Fortune"), Steam Cloud saves, Steam Leaderboards, Family Sharing, and single-player support. Accessibility options include save-anytime functionality, custom volume controls, and a design that's playable at your own pace without quick-time events.

## FAQ

**Is there a full-length One Million Coins official trailer beyond the 49-second clip?**

As of launch, the primary video on the Steam store page is a single short gameplay clip running about 49 seconds. It's backed up by ten screenshots that show the vault, the deposit station, and the upgrade interfaces. If a longer trailer arrives later, the store page is where it will appear first.

**How long does it take to finish the game?**

The developer states that most players should be able to work through the vault in around two hours. That's a deliberate design choice for a short, focused experience rather than an endless grind, and it's worth factoring into your purchase decision.

**Do you actually collect all 1,000,000 coins?**

That's the stated goal. The vault contains a finite pile of exactly one million coins, and the endgame is emptying it completely while restoring the bunker's infrastructure along the way. There's a clear finish line rather than an infinite scaling treadmill.

**Does the One Million Coins official trailer show the skill tree in detail?**

Not really. The clip focuses on the collecting and banking loop, while the 250+ node skill tree across seven upgrade paths is described in the written store copy. If build variety is what sells you on a game, read the store description carefully before buying.

**Is it worth $1.99?**

If you enjoy incremental games and want a tight, completable one, the price is low enough that a single good evening justifies it. If you need depth, story, or dozens of hours of content, the One Million Coins official trailer is honestly telling you what you're getting — and that's a rare and useful thing for a trailer to do.
