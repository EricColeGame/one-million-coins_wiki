## What Is One Million Coins? Gameplay and Core Loop

One Million Coins is a first-person incremental game about emptying a vault that contains exactly 1,000,000 coins. You start with empty pockets and nothing but your bare hands. You scoop gold off an enormous coin pile, haul it to a deposit station, convert it into spendable funds, and reinvest in better equipment.

The twist that separates it from a typical idle game is that you are physically present in the world. You walk, reach, carry, and deposit. Upgrades do not just tick numbers upward — they change how fast you move through the bunker and how much you can haul in a single trip.

| Loop step | What you do | What it unlocks |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Collect | Scoop coins by hand or with tools | Raw gold in your carry slot |
| 2. Transport | Walk your haul to the deposit station | Progress toward the next purchase |
| 3. Bank | Convert gold into spendable funds | Currency for upgrades |
| 4. Upgrade | Buy tools, bags, and skill nodes | Faster, larger, more efficient hauls |
| 5. Expand | Dig deeper and restore infrastructure | New zones and higher-tier tools |

The developer describes the game as a "short, focused experience" that most players should finish in about two hours. That is a deliberate design choice: a finite vault with a finite coin count means the game ends when you empty it, not when a live-service timer says so.

### The Skill Tree and Upgrade Paths

Progression runs through a skill tree with more than 250 nodes spread across seven upgrade paths. The named benefits include movement speed, reach, collection speed, tool strength, carry capacity, and deposit rate. Because the vault is finite, every node you buy is permanent progress toward a guaranteed endpoint.

Bags and backpacks expand how much you can carry per trip, while infrastructure projects restore deposit stations, cargo lifts, and conveyors that automate parts of the operation you used to do by hand.

## Progression Guide: Tools, Skill Tree, and Practical Tips

Because the One Million Coins release date is recent, the most useful advice right now comes from the game's own design and early player experience rather than from a deep meta. Still, the structure makes a few strategies obviously strong.

### Tool Progression at a Glance

| Tier | What you use | Practical effect |
|---|---|---|
| Starting tier | Bare hands | Slow, tiny hauls, zero cost |
| Early upgrades | Pouches and small bags | More coins per trip |
| Mid game | Backpacks and stronger tools | Bigger loads, faster scooping |
| Late game | High-powered collection tools | Pull in huge waves of coins |
| Endgame | Conveyors, cargo lifts, restored stations | Semi-automated operation |

### Tips That Actually Help

- **Bank often early.** Your carry capacity is the bottleneck at the start, not your collection speed. Short, frequent trips beat one heroic slow walk.
- **Prioritize capacity before speed.** A bigger bag multiplies every other upgrade you own. Speed only helps if you are not already full.
- **Do not ignore movement nodes.** The vault is a physical space. Walking time is real time, and movement upgrades quietly compound across hundreds of trips.
- **Restore infrastructure when it unlocks.** Cargo lifts and conveyors remove manual labor permanently. Anything that removes a repeated action is worth the coins.
- **Play at your own pace.** The game is explicitly designed to be playable at your own pace with no quick-time events and the ability to save anytime, so there is no reason to rush.

One structural point in the game's favor: with a fixed total of 1,000,000 coins, you can measure progress precisely. You always know exactly how much of the vault remains.
