## The Progression Loop: Collect, Bank, Buy, Repeat

Every meaningful decision in One Million Coins happens inside one loop. You scoop coins from the pile, carry them to a deposit station, convert them into spendable funds, and reinvest. The players who struggle are almost always breaking the loop somewhere.

| Loop stage | What you do | The mistake that costs you time |
|---|---|---|
| Collect | Scoop coins with your current tool | Swinging at coins you can't carry efficiently |
| Haul | Carry the load to a deposit station | Walking long routes before restoring infrastructure |
| Bank | Convert coins into spendable funds | Sitting on unbanked coins "just in case" |
| Upgrade | Buy tools, bags, and skill nodes | Spreading points evenly instead of fixing bottlenecks |
| Expand | Dig deeper and restore bunker systems | Pushing depth before capacity catches up |

Two principles follow from this table. First, bank early and bank often — unbanked coins are doing no work for you. Second, every upgrade should answer a question: what is slowing me down right now?

### Why the loop rewards "one bottleneck at a time" thinking

Incremental games tempt you to diversify. One Million Coins punishes that. If your trips are short because you run out of hands, extra deposit speed does nothing. If you're spending ten seconds walking per scoop, more capacity barely registers.

Community reports from early players echo the same pattern: identify the single slowest step in your current loop, fix it, then re-measure. Repeat for two hours and you'll be emptying bunker sections in single trips.

## One Million Coins Secrets for the Skill Tree

The skill tree is the biggest lever in the game — 250+ nodes spread across seven upgrade paths. The store page names movement, reach, collection speed, tool strength, capacity, and deposit rate as the main levers, with the seventh path rounding out the tree with support upgrades.

Here's the priority order that most players converge on.

| Upgrade path | What it improves | When it matters most |
|---|---|---|
| Capacity | How much you can carry per trip | Early — the single biggest multiplier |
| Collection speed | Coins gathered per second while scooping | Early to mid |
| Movement | Traversal speed through the bunker | Early to mid |
| Deposit rate | How fast banked coins convert | Mid game |
| Reach | How far you can grab coins without repositioning | Mid to late |
| Tool strength | Raw power of each unlocked tool | Mid to late |

### Early game: fix capacity first

Your first hour should feel like a snowball. Capacity nodes and bag upgrades multiply the value of every single trip you take, which means they multiply the value of every future upgrade too. Collection speed comes second, because faster scooping only helps if you can actually hold what you scoop.

### Mid game: stop walking

Once loads are reasonable, movement and deposit rate become the hidden time sinks. Long walks between the pile and the station, and slow conversion at the station, are the two biggest sources of dead time. This is also the point where restoring infrastructure starts to pay for itself.

### Late game: convert power into volume

Late in a run, tool strength and reach unlock the fantasy the store page promises — tools that pull in huge waves of coins and turn the vault into a personal gold rush. By then, your capacity and movement are already solved, so raw power translates directly into coins per minute.

## Capacity, Tools, and Infrastructure Secrets

If there's one non-obvious **One Million Coins** secret worth repeating, it's this: your carry system is a progression track, not a piece of gear. You start with bare hands, graduate to pouches and backpacks, and then stack skill-based capacity on top.

| Carry stage | How it feels | Best paired with |
|---|---|---|
| Bare hands | Tiny scoops, constant trips | Collection speed nodes |
| Pouches | First real jump in trip value | Capacity nodes |
| Backpacks | Loads that make upgrades feel cheap | Movement speed |
| Capacity skill nodes | Stacking multipliers on top of gear | Deposit rate |
| Late-game tools | Volume collection, minimal repositioning | Reach and tool strength |

Infrastructure follows the same logic. Deposit stations, cargo lifts, conveyors, and general bunker restoration are not decoration — each one removes a specific piece of friction from your route. A restored lift can turn a fifteen-second walk into a two-second ride, and that time compounds across hundreds of trips.

A practical tip: before you buy a big tool upgrade, ask whether restored infrastructure would make your current tool fast enough. Early on, the answer is often yes.

## One Million Coins Secrets for a Fast, Complete Clear

The stated goal is simple: collect every last coin and empty the bunker completely. With a two-hour target, pacing matters as much as optimization. Use this milestone table as a rough self-check — if you're behind, your bottleneck is in the column on the right.

| Milestone | Roughly where you should be | If you're behind, fix |
|---|---|---|
| First tool upgrade purchased | First few minutes | Stop hoarding coins — bank everything |
| Bags and pouches equipped | Early run | Prioritize capacity over speed |
| Infrastructure restored on your main route | Mid run | Movement and deposit rate |
| Skill tree heavily invested | Mid to late run | Stop spreading points evenly |
| Late-game tools online | Final stretch | Tool strength and reach |
| Vault emptied | Around the two-hour mark | Re-check your loop for dead time |

A few extra habits that separate fast runs from slow ones:

- **Bank before you explore.** Depth is worthless if you can't fund the tools to exploit it.
- **Rebuild your route after every infrastructure unlock.** Old paths become obsolete fast.
- **Check achievements as a progress tracker.** With 16 Steam achievements, they double as a checklist of milestones you may have skipped.
- **Don't chase leaderboards on your first run.** Learn the vault, then optimize the route.
- **Save and quit freely.** Save Anytime is supported, so there's no penalty for breaking a run into sessions.

Player experience suggests the biggest time loss isn't slow collecting — it's walking. Watch your own footage mentally: if you're spending more time in transit than scooping, your next upgrade should be movement or infrastructure, not raw power.

## FAQ

**How long does One Million Coins take to finish?**
The store page states most players should work through the vault in around two hours. Your first run will likely run longer while you learn the layout; a second run with these progression tips can be noticeably tighter.

**Are there hidden coins or secret areas I should hunt for?**
Nothing in the official material documents hidden collectibles. The vault is finite at exactly 1,000,000 coins, so the real **One Million Coins secrets** are systemic: bottleneck-first spending, capacity stacking, and route efficiency. Don't waste time searching for invisible pickups.

**Should I spread skill points across all seven paths?**
No. Spreading points evenly is the most common progression mistake. Fund the single step that's slowing you down, confirm the improvement, then move to the next bottleneck. The 250+ node tree is deep enough that you'll still touch most paths by the end.

**Is One Million Coins worth playing if I only have a couple of hours?**
That's exactly the design intent. It's a short, focused experience with a clear ending rather than an endless grind — which makes it a good fit for players who want a complete incremental arc in one or two sittings. The launch discount has ended, but it remains a budget-priced indie release.

**Does it work on Steam Deck?**
Compatibility was listed as Unknown at launch. It supports Xbox controllers and full English subtitles, and the accessibility notes emphasize playing at your own pace, but verify current Steam Deck status before buying if that's your main platform.

The through-line across all of these tips is the same: One Million Coins rewards players who measure their loop instead of grinding through it. Fix capacity, kill dead time, and let the vault empty itself.
