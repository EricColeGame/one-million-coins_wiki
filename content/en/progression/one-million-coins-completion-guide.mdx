Finishing **One Million Coins** sounds straightforward — scoop a finite pile of gold until the vault is empty — but the difference between a comfortable two-hour clear and a dragging four-hour grind comes down to the order you spend your money. This **One Million Coins completion guide** breaks down upgrade priorities, skill tree routing, and infrastructure unlocks that turn a slow hand-scooping routine into a self-feeding gold rush. The vault is finite by design, which means every wasted purchase is time you can't get back.

## What "Completion" Really Means in One Million Coins

Unlike an endless idle game, this one has a hard finish line. The vault contains exactly 1,000,000 coins, and the developer describes the whole experience as a short, focused run that most players can work through in roughly two hours. Completion therefore means four separate goals, not just one.

| Completion goal | What it takes | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Empty the vault | Bank every one of the 1,000,000 coins | The core finish line |
| Restore the bunker | Rebuild deposit stations, cargo lifts, and conveyors | Removes manual hauling bottlenecks |
| Max out the skill tree | Spend across 250+ nodes on seven upgrade paths | Multiplies speed, reach, and capacity |
| Unlock all 16 achievements | Hit banking and upgrade milestones | The recognized 100% marker |

A useful mindset for the **One Million Coins completion guide** is to treat the vault as a throughput problem rather than a collection problem. You will always have coins to pick up; the question is how many trips you need and how fast each trip moves.

## The Core Loop: Bank, Upgrade, Repeat

The loop never changes, but its speed does. You collect gold directly from the enormous coin pile, carry it to a deposit station, convert it into spendable funds, then spend those funds on tools, bags, and skill nodes. Every upgrade should shorten the next loop.

Where players usually stall is buying power before efficiency. A bigger tool that takes the same number of trips to justify is worse than a capacity upgrade that halves your walking. Here is how the phases break down in practice.

| Phase | Your setup | Main bottleneck | Where to spend |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Opening | Bare hands | Carry capacity | First pouch, basic movement |
| Early | Pouches | Number of trips | Capacity and deposit rate |
| Mid | Backpacks | Collection and travel speed | Collection speed, tool strength |
| Late | Powered tools | Raw throughput | Tool power, deposit efficiency |
| Endgame | Late-game rigs | Scattered leftover coins | Cleanup sweep across the vault |

Once your haul size outpaces your collection speed, you are officially in the mid-game — and that is where the build starts to matter.

## Skill Tree Priorities: 250+ Nodes, Seven Paths

The skill tree is the heart of the progression system. It spans more than 250 nodes across seven upgrade paths, and the store description names six of them directly: movement, reach, collection speed, tool strength, capacity, and deposit rate.

Because there is no published information about respecs, treat early points as semi-permanent and favor generic stats that help every build. The table below is a safe ordering for a first run.

| Priority | Upgrade path | When to invest | Payoff |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Capacity | Immediately | Fewer trips per coin load |
| 2 | Collection speed | After your first bag | Faster scooping at the pile |
| 3 | Movement | Once loads get heavy | Shorter round trips |
| 4 | Deposit rate | Early mid-game | More funds per haul |
| 5 | Reach | Before digging deeper | Grab coins without repositioning |
| 6 | Tool strength | Before major tool purchases | Keeps new equipment scaling |

A common mistake in any **One Million Coins completion guide** discussion is dumping points into tool strength early, when you are still limited by how much you can carry. Strength multiplies a tool you do not yet have; capacity multiplies the hands you already own.

### How to Read the Tree Without Wasting Points

- Take the cheapest node that removes your current bottleneck, then stop.
- Re-check your bottleneck after every two or three purchases.
- If a branch only helps after a specific tool unlock, delay it until you own that tool.
- Bank a small reserve so a newly unlocked path is affordable the moment it appears.

## Bags, Tools, and Capacity Progression

Carry capacity is the single most important stat in the first third of the run, and the game gives you a clear ladder for it: hands, pouches, backpacks, then progressively stronger collection tools. Each tier exists to make the previous one feel slow.

| Gear tier | What it changes | When to buy |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bare hands | One small handful per scoop | Starting state |
| Pouches | A modest capacity bump | First few hundred coins |
| Backpacks | Large loads, noticeably fewer returns | After roughly the first chunk of the vault |
| Powered collectors | Coins arrive in waves | Mid-game, once capacity is comfortable |
| Late-game tools | Vault-wide pulls and rapid sweeps | Final third of the pile |

Player experience from early reviews suggests the pacing feels best when you buy a new bag the moment it is affordable, then immediately sink the next few payouts into skill nodes that support it. Do not save for a distant tool while your current trips take twice as long as they should.

## Restoring the Bunker: Infrastructure Unlocks

Digging deeper is not only about reaching more coins — it is about repairing the bunker itself. Deposit stations, cargo lifts, and conveyors all exist to cut the distance between where coins sit and where they get banked.

| Infrastructure | What it does | Priority |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Extra deposit stations | Shorten the walk from pile to bank | High, early |
| Cargo lifts | Move hauls between bunker levels | High, once you dig downward |
| Conveyors | Automate coin movement | Medium-high in the late game |
| Deeper excavation | Opens new sections of the pile | Do it as soon as your tools allow |

A practical rule: never excavate deeper than your hauling setup can support. Opening a fresh section of the vault with slow travel just creates a longer walk. Fix the route first, then expand into it.

## Achievements and Endgame Cleanup

There are 16 Steam achievements, and the visible examples — Pocket Change, An Honest Day's Work, Change Management, and A Small Fortune — suggest a mix of banking milestones and upgrade milestones. That means a full completion run rewards both emptying the vault and building the operation that does it.

A few quality-of-life features make the final stretch painless. Save Anytime and Steam Cloud support mean you can stop mid-run without losing progress, and Steam leaderboards give you something to compare once the vault is empty. The developer also notes the game is playable at your own pace, so there is no pressure to rush the last pockets of coins.

For the cleanup phase, sweep from the top down. High-level areas hold the coins you skipped early when your reach was short, and they are usually the fastest to clear once your late-game tools are online.

## FAQ

**How long does a full completion take?**
The developer states that most players should work through the vault in around two hours. A relaxed first run with exploration will run longer, while a second run focused on the **One Million Coins completion guide** priorities above is usually faster.

**Do I need to max the entire skill tree to finish?**
No. Emptying all 1,000,000 coins is the main goal, but achievement hunters will want to push deeper into the 250+ node tree for the upgrade-related unlocks.

**What should I upgrade first?**
Capacity, then collection speed, then movement. Those three remove the biggest time sinks before any tool power matters.

**Can I take my time?**
Yes. Save Anytime, Steam Cloud, and a design built around playing at your own pace mean you can bank a haul, quit, and pick the run back up later without penalty.
