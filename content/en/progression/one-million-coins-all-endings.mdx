## What "All Endings" Really Means in One Million Coins

One Million Coins looks like a simple coin-scooping game until you notice the fine print: the vault contains exactly 1,000,000 coins, and once they're gone, they're gone. That finite design is why so many players go looking for One Million Coins all endings — they want to know whether the game branches, whether there's a secret finale, and whether finishing the vault locks them out of anything.

Here's the honest answer up front. One Million Coins doesn't have branching narrative endings the way a story-driven RPG does. What it has is a single definitive finish line — an empty bunker — plus several genuinely different ways to reach it. Understanding One Million Coins all endings is really about understanding those routes, because the path you pick changes how long the run takes, which achievements you unlock, and how much of the skill tree you actually get to enjoy.

This guide breaks down each finish type, the upgrade paths that shape them, and the mistakes that stall a run.

## The True Ending: How to Empty the Vault

The true ending is straightforward to describe and satisfying to execute. You start with empty pockets and bare hands, scoop coins manually, carry them to a deposit station, and convert them into spendable funds. Repeat until the pile is gone.

What separates a smooth run from a grind is sequencing. The vault is layered, and your reach, movement speed, and capacity determine how deep you can efficiently work. Pushing deeper before upgrading your bag is the single most common cause of a stalled run.

### Phase 1: Early Vault (Hands and Pouches)

- Bank every single haul immediately — idle coins do nothing for you
- Buy capacity upgrades before speed upgrades; fewer trips beats faster trips early on
- Unlock pouches and backpacks as soon as your balance allows
- Resist the urge to spread skill points across every path

### Phase 2: Mid Vault (Tools and Reach)

This is where the run opens up. Tool strength and reach upgrades let you pull coins from a wider radius, which dramatically cuts the walking between the pile and the deposit station. Deposit rate upgrades compound with this — a faster deposit means less standing around.

### Phase 3: Deep Bunker (Infrastructure and Automation)

Late game is about restoration. Deposit stations, cargo lifts, conveyors, and bunker infrastructure can all be brought back online, and each one removes a manual step from your loop. By the final stretch, you should be pulling in huge waves of coins with minimal input.

| Phase | Primary Focus | Signal You're Ready to Move On |
|---|---|---|
| Early Vault | Carry capacity, basic pouches | You fill your bag in under a minute |
| Mid Vault | Tool strength, reach, deposit rate | You're clearing a layer in a few trips |
| Deep Bunker | Infrastructure, cargo lifts, conveyors | Manual scooping is a rounding error |
| Final Cleanup | Reach and movement | You're hunting stragglers, not piles |

## Skill Tree Builds That Decide How You Finish

The skill tree is the heart of the progression system — over 250 nodes spread across seven upgrade paths. Which paths you prioritize determines your ending type more than anything else.

| Path | What It Improves | Priority for Fast Finish | Priority for Cozy Finish |
|---|---|---|---|
| Movement | Traversal speed through the bunker | High | Medium |
| Reach | How far you can grab coins | Very High | Medium |
| Collection Speed | Coins per second while scooping | High | Medium |
| Tool Strength | Effectiveness of each tool tier | Very High | High |
| Capacity | How much you can carry per trip | Very High | High |
| Deposit Rate | How fast hauls convert to funds | High | Low |
| Bunker Infrastructure | Restored stations, lifts, conveyors | Medium | Very High |

A practical rule from player experience: for the first third of the run, put nearly everything into Capacity and Collection Speed. Coins in your bag are worth more than coins on the floor, and the early vault is dense enough that raw scooping speed pays for itself fast.

Once your bag stops being the bottleneck, pivot hard into Reach and Tool Strength. Community reports consistently point to Reach as the most underrated stat — it quietly saves more time than any movement upgrade because it eliminates repositioning.

Save infrastructure investment for the back half. Restoring cargo lifts and conveyors feels amazing, but doing it too early means you're spending coins on convenience instead of throughput.

## Achievements, Run Times, and Mistakes to Avoid

### The Achievement Ending

One Million Coins ships with 16 Steam achievements. Four of them are visible on the store listing — Pocket Change, An Honest Day's Work, Change Management, and A Small Fortune — which suggests a progression ladder running from your first banked haul up to a serious fortune milestone.

| Achievement | Likely Focus | When It Typically Pops |
|---|---|---|
| Pocket Change | First banked coins | First few minutes |
| An Honest Day's Work | Sustained manual collecting | Early vault |
| Change Management | Spending on upgrades | Early to mid vault |
| A Small Fortune | Accumulated balance | Mid to late vault |
| Remaining 12 | Mixed milestones | Spread across the full run |

Note: the achievement names are confirmed on the Steam listing, but exact unlock thresholds aren't published. Treat the "likely focus" column as informed inference rather than an official requirement list.

The important strategic point is that achievement hunting and vault clearing are compatible. Almost everything you'd do for a fast, efficient finish also advances the milestone achievements, so you rarely need a dedicated second playthrough.

### How Long Each Route Takes

| Route | Estimated Time | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Efficient Vault Clear | ~1.5–2 hours | Requires disciplined skill allocation |
| Standard Vault Clear | ~2 hours | The developer's stated target for most players |
| Achievement-Focused Clear | 2–4 hours | Extra time only if you chase stragglers |

The developer describes One Million Coins as a short, focused experience, with most players expected to work through the vault in around two hours. That's a genuinely tight runtime for an incremental game, and it means a single sitting is realistic.

### Mistakes That Delay Your Ending

- **Buying tools before capacity.** You'll carry the same tiny load faster and go nowhere.
- **Ignoring the deposit station.** Unbanked coins can't buy upgrades, and upgrades are the entire game.
- **Spreading skill points evenly.** Seven paths sounds like a buffet; it's actually a trap. Commit.
- **Over-investing in infrastructure early.** Lifts and conveyors are endgame multipliers, not early accelerators.
- **Not using Save Anytime.** The game supports saving at will, so there's no reason to lose a good run to a bad decision.

## FAQ

**Does One Million Coins have multiple endings?**
No — there's one canonical finish, which is collecting all 1,000,000 coins and emptying the bunker. When people ask about One Million Coins all endings, they're usually asking about the different completion styles: the efficient speed clear, the achievement run, and the relaxed infrastructure-first playthrough.

**Can I miss the ending permanently?**
No. The vault is finite but the game is designed to be played at your own pace, and Save Anytime is supported. There's no soft lock that stops you from eventually clearing every last coin.

**What's the fastest way to see the ending?**
Prioritize Capacity, Collection Speed, Tool Strength, and Reach in that rough order, bank every haul immediately, and delay infrastructure spending until the deep bunker. That's the route that gets most players to an empty vault in around two hours.

**Is it worth doing a second run?**
Yes, if you enjoy optimization. A replay with a focused build plays very differently from an exploratory first run, and the skill tree has enough nodes that you'll likely use a completely different slice of it the second time around.
