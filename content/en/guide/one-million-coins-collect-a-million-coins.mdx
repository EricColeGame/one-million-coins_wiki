## What Is One Million Coins?

Most incremental games ask you to click a button and watch a counter climb. One Million Coins takes a different approach: it drops you into an underground vault with bare hands and exactly 1,000,000 gold pieces, then dares you to carry every last one out. If you want to collect a million coins without wasting hours on inefficient upgrades, the order you buy things in matters more than how fast you click.

This guide breaks down the core loop, the best upgrade priorities, and the late-game infrastructure that turns a slow manual grind into a self-running gold rush.

One Million Coins is a short, first-person incremental mining game from solo developer and publisher ShoXx. Rather than abstract menus, you physically scoop gold from an enormous pile, walk it to a deposit station, and convert it into spendable funds. Because the vault is finite — every coin you carry out is one fewer left behind — progress is measurable in a way that endless idle games rarely achieve. The [official One Million Coins page on Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/5258340/One_Million_Coins/) describes it as a focused experience most players can finish in around two hours.

## The Core Loop: Scoop, Bank, Upgrade

Everything in One Million Coins revolves around a repeating four-beat rhythm. You grab coins, haul them to a deposit station, bank the haul into spendable funds, and immediately reinvest those funds into something that makes the next trip shorter or heavier. Understanding that rhythm is the difference between a two-hour clear and a four-hour slog.

| Stage | What You Do | What You Get |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Scoop | Collect gold directly from the pile with your hands or a tool | A partial load of coins |
| 2. Haul | Walk the load to the nearest deposit station | Coins in transit |
| 3. Bank | Deposit the haul and convert it to funds | Spendable currency |
| 4. Reinvest | Buy capacity, tools, or skill tree nodes | Faster, bigger next haul |

The single most important rule: **never walk a trip empty-handed.** Movement time is the real cost in this game. If you're crossing the bunker to buy an upgrade, you should be carrying a full load in that direction. Players who internalize this early report noticeably smoother progression, because dead travel time is the one resource no upgrade can refund.

A second principle follows from the first: your carry capacity determines how much value each trip delivers. A trip takes roughly the same number of seconds whether you're holding 20 coins or 200. Doubling capacity effectively halves the time cost per coin.

## Best Upgrade Order to Collect a Million Coins Faster

Because the skill tree spans 250+ nodes across seven upgrade paths, it's easy to spread your funds thin. The paths improve movement, reach, collection speed, tool strength, capacity, and deposit rate — and each one feels tempting the moment you can afford it. In practice, a disciplined order beats scattered spending.

### Early Game: Capacity Before Speed

Your first purchases should target carrying capacity through pouches and backpacks, then the corresponding skill nodes. More coins per trip compounds immediately, while raw speed upgrades only shave seconds off a route you're still learning.

### Mid Game: Movement, Reach, and Collection Speed

Once you can haul a respectable load, movement speed and reach start paying off. Reach in particular reduces the repositioning you do while scooping, which is where a surprising amount of time disappears. Collection speed smooths out the actual scooping animation and keeps your hands busy.

### Late Game: Tool Power and Deposit Rate

Late in a run, tool strength lets you break into packed clusters of coins that would otherwise take forever to clear by hand. Deposit rate matters most once your hauls are enormous, because standing at the station becomes the bottleneck.

| Priority | Upgrade Path | Why It Pays Off | When to Buy |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Capacity (pouches, backpacks) | More value per identical trip | First 10 minutes |
| 2 | Movement speed | Shorter round trips, fewer wasted seconds | Early |
| 3 | Reach | Less repositioning while scooping | Early to mid |
| 4 | Collection speed | Faster scooping, steadier coin flow | Mid |
| 5 | Tool strength | Cracks packed clusters and deep pockets | Mid |
| 6 | Deposit rate | Clears big hauls without waiting | Mid to late |
| 7 | Infrastructure | Automates transport entirely | Late |

## Common Mistakes That Slow Down a Vault Clear

A few habits reliably add an hour to a run. The good news is that all of them are easy to fix once you notice them.

| Mistake | Why It Hurts | The Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Buying tools before capacity | Expensive tools move the same small load | Max capacity first, tools second |
| Empty-handed trips | Pure wasted movement time | Always carry a load when traveling |
| Scattering skill points | No path reaches a meaningful threshold | Commit to two or three paths at a time |
| Ignoring deposit stations | Long walks to a distant bank | Restore nearby stations early |
| Hoarding funds | Unspent money does nothing for you | Reinvest as soon as an upgrade is affordable |
| Skipping reach upgrades | Constant repositioning while scooping | Take reach nodes in the early-mid game |

If you only remember one thing from this guide: treat your carry capacity as the engine and everything else as fuel. Capacity is what converts every second of walking into coins.

## Restoring the Bunker: Turning Manual Work Into Infrastructure

The deeper you excavate, the more of the bunker's broken infrastructure you can bring back online. Deposit stations, cargo lifts, conveyors, and other bunker systems shift work off your shoulders and onto the environment itself. This is the point where One Million Coins stops feeling like a manual chore and starts feeling like an operation you're building.

| Infrastructure | What It Does for You | Priority |
|---|---|---|
| Deposit stations | Shortens the banking leg of every trip | High |
| Cargo lifts | Moves loads vertically through the bunker | Medium |
| Conveyors | Transports coins without you carrying them | Medium to high |
| Bunker systems | Restores broader vault functionality | Situational |

Community reports from the game's early Steam reviews — both of the two reviews currently posted are positive — suggest that the transition from hands-on scooping to automated collection is the most satisfying arc in the game. That tracks with the design: the game is explicitly built around becoming a "gold-collecting machine," with late-game tools that pull in huge waves of coins at once.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How long does it take to collect a million coins in One Million Coins?**
The developer states that most players can work through the vault in around two hours. First-time players who experiment with the skill tree may take longer, while a focused second run with a capacity-first build can be considerably faster.

**Do I actually have to collect all 1,000,000 coins?**
The vault contains exactly one million coins, and the end goal is to empty the bunker completely. You don't need perfect efficiency to finish, but the game is designed so that late-game tools let you sweep up large waves of coins rather than picking them up one by one.

**Is One Million Coins worth buying?**
At $2.49 — currently discounted to $1.99 during the introductory offer — it's a low-risk purchase for fans of incremental games who want something hands-on rather than menu-driven. Early player experience on Steam is limited but positive, so treat those two reviews as a small sample rather than a consensus.

**Can my PC run One Million Coins?**
It's a lightweight release with modest requirements. Minimum specs call for Windows 10 (64-bit), an Intel Core i5-8400 or AMD Ryzen 5 2600, 8 GB of RAM, a GTX 1060 or RX 580, and 2 GB of storage. Recommended specs step up to 16 GB of RAM and an RTX 2060 or RX 6600.

## Final Thoughts

One Million Coins succeeds because it makes an abstract number feel physical. You aren't watching a counter tick toward a million — you're hauling it, trip by trip, until the pile is gone and the bunker runs itself. Buy capacity before speed, keep your hands full on every walk, commit to a few skill paths instead of all seven, and restore deposit stations the moment they become available. Do that, and you'll collect a million coins in a single comfortable sitting rather than a weekend-long grind.
