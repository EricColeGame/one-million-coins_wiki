## Why One Million Coins Is Shorter Than It Looks

This one asks for an afternoon. **One Million Coins** is a first-person mining game built around a single, finite promise: a vault contains exactly 1,000,000 coins, and your job is to carry every last one of them to a deposit station. There is no endless prestige loop and no second bunker waiting after this one — which is exactly why a good **One Million Coins gameplay walkthrough** is worth reading before you load in.

The developer, ShoXx, states that most players should be able to work through the vault in around **two hours**. That short runway changes everything about how you should play. In a long idle game you can afford to experiment; here, a wasted early hour is half your run.

## The Core Loop: Collect, Bank, Upgrade, Repeat

Every run in One Million Coins follows the same three-beat rhythm, and understanding it is the foundation of any successful One Million Coins gameplay walkthrough.

1. **Collect.** You physically scoop or vacuum coins out of the pile with whatever tool you currently own.
2. **Bank.** You carry the load to a deposit station, which converts raw gold into spendable funds.
3. **Upgrade.** You spend those funds on better tools, bigger bags, or skill tree nodes — which makes step one faster.

The trap for new players is treating coins in your hands as progress. They are not. Coins sitting in a pouch are doing nothing for you; coins in the deposit station buy the tool that triples your next haul. Bank early, bank often, and never walk past a deposit station with a full load just because you want one more scoop.

This is also a genuinely low-pressure game. The store page lists accessibility features like "Save Anytime" and "Playable at Your Own Pace," and there are no quick-time events to worry about. If you only have twenty minutes, you can still make meaningful progress.

## One Million Coins Gameplay Walkthrough: Four Phases of a Full Clear

The cleanest way to plan a run is to break it into four phases. The timings below are a suggested pacing based on the game's stated two-hour runtime, not measured data — your mileage will vary depending on how efficiently you bank.

| Phase | Time window | Primary focus | Goal |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Bare hands | 0–15 min | Manual scooping | Fund your first pouch |
| 2. Capacity | 15–50 min | Bags, movement, carry nodes | Longer trips, fewer returns |
| 3. Tooling up | 50–100 min | Tool strength, reach, deposit rate | Big multi-coin pulls |
| 4. The sweep | 100–120 min | Late-game tools + infrastructure | Empty the bunker |

### Phase 1: Bare Hands

You start with empty pockets and nothing but your hands. Loads are tiny and trips are constant, which is intentional — this phase exists to teach you the layout of the bunker and the location of the first deposit station. Do not overthink it. Scoop, walk, bank, repeat until you can afford your first upgrade.

### Phase 2: Capacity and Mobility

Once pouches and backpacks enter the picture, the math flips. A single trip that carries three times as much is worth more than a tool that collects three times as fast, because travel time is the hidden cost of every haul. Invest in carry capacity and movement speed first.

### Phase 3: Tool Power and Deposit Efficiency

With a decent bag, your bottleneck shifts from walking to scooping. This is where tool strength and collection speed nodes start paying for themselves, and where deposit rate upgrades reduce the time you lose at the station itself. Reach upgrades are quietly excellent here too — they let you pull coins without repositioning.

### Phase 4: The Sweep

Late in the run you unlock high-powered tools that pull in huge waves of coins rather than individual ones. Restoring bunker infrastructure — cargo lifts, conveyors, and additional deposit stations — turns the vault into something closer to an automated gold rush. This is the phase where the coin count visibly collapses.

## Upgrade Priorities: Skill Tree, Tools, and Carry Capacity

The skill tree is the heart of the game, with more than 250 nodes spread across seven upgrade paths. The store description names six benefits explicitly, and they map neatly onto a priority order.

| Path | What it improves | Priority | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Capacity | Load size | Highest early | Cuts the number of trips per coin |
| Movement | Travel speed | Highest early | Travel is the biggest hidden time sink |
| Reach | Collection range | High | Fewer repositioning steps per scoop |
| Collection speed | Scoop rate | Medium | Pays off once trips are long |
| Tool strength | Pull power | Medium | Unlocks multi-coin grabs |
| Deposit rate | Banking speed | Low early, high late | Matters most when hauls get huge |

A simple rule: **if it reduces the number of trips, buy it now; if it only improves a single trip, buy it later.** Players who follow that heuristic generally report a smoother curve than those who dump everything into raw tool power first.

### Tools and Bags, Tier by Tier

| Tier | Example gear | Relative load | Buy when |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | Bare hands | Smallest | Start of run |
| 1 | First pouch | Small | After your first few deposits |
| 2 | Backpack | Medium | When trips feel constant |
| 3 | Upgraded collection tools | Medium-large | When scooping is the bottleneck |
| 4 | Late-game powered tools | Massive | Final phase of the run |

Notice that gear and skill nodes overlap deliberately. A bigger bag with no movement upgrades just means you walk slower with more weight; a fast character with a tiny bag just means more round trips. Balance the two.

## Completion Tips, Achievements, and Performance Notes

There are 16 Steam achievements, and their names — **Pocket Change**, **An Honest Day's Work**, **Change Management**, and **A Small Fortune** among them — suggest a spread from early milestones to near-total vault clears. Because the game supports Steam Cloud and Save Anytime, you can chip away at them across multiple sessions without losing progress.

A few habits that consistently help:

- **Bank before you explore.** Discovering a new area is not progress until the coins are deposited.
- **Restore infrastructure as soon as it appears.** Lifts and conveyors move coins for you; that is free throughput.
- **Do not hoard for a big purchase.** Buying two mid-tier upgrades usually beats waiting for one expensive one.
- **Use leaderboards as a benchmark, not a target.** They are a fun comparison, not a requirement.

On the technical side, requirements are modest. The minimum spec calls for an Intel Core i5-8400 or AMD Ryzen 5 2600, 8 GB of RAM, and a GTX 1060 or RX 580, with only 2 GB of storage. A recommended build with 16 GB of RAM and an RTX 2060 or RX 6600 will run it comfortably. Steam Deck compatibility is listed as unknown, so handheld players should check before buying.

One caveat worth stating plainly: early Steam reviews are thin — the store page showed just a couple of user reviews at the time of writing, both positive. Treat any tier list, including this one, as **player experience** rather than settled consensus, and adjust to how your own run feels.

## FAQ: One Million Coins Gameplay Walkthrough Questions

**How long does a full One Million Coins gameplay walkthrough take?**
The developer estimates around two hours for most players. A focused run that banks constantly and prioritizes capacity and movement upgrades can land on the shorter side, while a relaxed exploration-focused run will run longer.

**Can I finish the vault without upgrading?**
Practically, no. Bare-hand loads are small enough that collecting a million coins that way would take an unreasonable amount of time. Upgrades are the progression system, not an optional extra.

**Is there a New Game Plus or endless mode?**
Nothing in the official description suggests one. The vault is described as finite, containing exactly 1,000,000 coins, and the stated goal is emptying it completely. Treat the run as a complete, self-contained experience.

**What should I upgrade first in One Million Coins?**
Carry capacity and movement speed. Both reduce the number of trips you make, and trip count — not scoop speed — is the real bottleneck for most of the run. Tool strength and deposit rate become the priority once your trips are already long.

The bottom line: One Million Coins is a tight, two-hour incremental game that rewards planning over grinding. Bank every haul, upgrade anything that shortens a trip, and the vault empties faster than you would expect.
