One Million Coins is a short, first-person incremental game about emptying a vault that holds exactly 1,000,000 gold coins — by hand at first, then with an escalating arsenal of mining tools. It launched on Steam on October 13, 2026, from solo developer and publisher ShoXx, and it is priced like a snack rather than a full meal. If you are searching for One Million Coins how to play guidance, this guide covers the core loop, the upgrade order that saves the most time, and the small mistakes that keep new players scooping coins for hours longer than they need to.

The hook is that the game is honest about its own scale. There really are one million coins in the bunker, and the finish line is a completely empty vault. Understanding how to play One Million Coins well is mostly about understanding bottlenecks — what is slowing you down right now, and which upgrade removes that specific wall.

## What Kind of Game Is One Million Coins?

Despite a few community tags that suggest otherwise, this is not an action roguelike and it is not an idle game you leave running in a browser tab. It is an *active* incremental game played in first person. You physically walk to the coin pile, scoop gold, carry it to a deposit station, and spend the proceeds on better equipment.

The developer describes it as a short, focused experience, and the pacing reflects that. Most players should be able to work through the vault in roughly two hours. That matters for how you approach it: this is a game you finish in one or two sittings, not a months-long grind.

## The Core Loop: One Million Coins How to Play Step by Step

Every session follows the same repeating cycle. Once you internalize it, the game becomes a rhythm game about route efficiency rather than a clicking game.

| Step | Action | Why It Matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Scoop coins from the pile | Collection speed and tool strength determine how fast gold enters your hands |
| 2 | Carry the load to a deposit station | Your carry capacity caps how much you can move per trip |
| 3 | Bank the haul into spendable funds | Nothing can be purchased until gold is deposited |
| 4 | Buy tools, pouches, and backpacks | Equipment raises the ceiling on every future trip |
| 5 | Spend skill points in the tree | Passive multipliers compound on top of your gear |
| 6 | Push deeper and restore infrastructure | New areas unlock new deposit points and automation |
| 7 | Repeat until the vault is empty | The pile is finite — every trip permanently reduces it |

The single most important concept in the early game is **carry capacity**. New players instinctively pour money into collection speed, then wonder why their runs feel slow. Scooping fast is meaningless if you are jogging back to the deposit station every fifteen seconds. Capacity first, speed second.

### How the Deposit Station Actually Works

The deposit station converts raw gold into funds you can spend. Because banking is a separate step from collecting, a long walk back to the station is effectively dead time. As you restore the bunker, you unlock additional infrastructure — deposit stations, cargo lifts, and conveyors — that shorten that walk and eventually move gold for you. Prioritize restoring infrastructure the moment it becomes available; it is the closest thing the game has to automation.

## Early Game Upgrade Priorities

Here is a practical order of operations for your first thirty to forty minutes, based on how the bottlenecks actually stack up.

| Priority | Upgrade | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Pouches and backpacks | Directly increases coins per trip, the hardest early cap |
| 2 | Collection speed | Reduces time spent scooping at the pile |
| 3 | Movement speed | Cuts travel time on both legs of the round trip |
| 4 | Reach | Lets you grab coins without repositioning constantly |
| 5 | Tool strength | Unlocks faster and larger scoops as better tools appear |
| 6 | Deposit rate | Speeds up banking once your loads get genuinely huge |

A useful rule of thumb: if a trip takes you longer to walk than to fill, you need capacity or movement. If you fill instantly and stand there waiting for the deposit animation, you need deposit rate or a better tool. Diagnose the bottleneck, then buy the fix.

## Mastering the 250+ Node Skill Tree

The skill tree is where One Million Coins stops being a simple scooping sim. It spans more than 250 nodes across seven upgrade paths, and it improves movement, reach, collection speed, tool strength, capacity, and deposit rate. Because the tree is finite and your playthrough is short, you cannot fill everything — you have to choose a build.

| Skill Path | What It Improves | Best For |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Movement | Walking and traversal speed | Long routes, deep bunker sections |
| Reach | Pickup range from the pile | Wide, awkward coin spreads |
| Collection Speed | Coins gathered per action | Dense piles near a station |
| Tool Strength | Power of your equipped tool | Mid and late game burst hauls |
| Capacity | Maximum carry weight | Every stage, but especially early |
| Deposit Rate | Speed of converting gold to funds | Late game when hauls are enormous |

### Should You Specialize or Spread Out?

Community reports from early players suggest that spreading points evenly is the safest approach for a first run, because the game keeps shifting which stat is your bottleneck. Specializing into capacity and movement creates a "long haul" build that rewards big, infrequent trips. Specializing into tool strength and collection speed creates a "fast scoop" build that shines once you have restored conveyors and cargo lifts to handle transport for you.

Either works. What does not work is ignoring capacity entirely — it is the stat that quietly governs the pace of the entire first hour.

## Tips for a Faster, Cleaner Clear

- **Bank before you browse.** Never shop with unbanked gold. Deposit first so you know exactly what you can afford.
- **Restore infrastructure early.** Cargo lifts and conveyors convert manual labor into passive throughput.
- **Do not hoard skill points.** The tree is finite and the run is only about two hours. Unspent points do nothing.
- **Buy the next tool before the next cosmetic.** Tools change your throughput; everything else is noise.
- **Use the "save anytime" feature.** One Million Coins supports saving whenever you want, so you can stop mid-haul without losing progress.
- **Play at your own pace.** The game is explicitly designed without quick-time events and is playable at whatever tempo you enjoy.

### Common Beginner Mistakes

| Mistake | Why It Hurts | Fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Over-investing in collection speed | You fill up faster but still walk slowly | Balance with capacity and movement |
| Skipping the deposit station | Unbanked gold cannot buy anything | Bank every single haul |
| Ignoring the skill tree | Free permanent multipliers go unused | Spend points immediately |
| Digging too deep too early | You stretch your route before you have the tools | Clear shallow areas first |
| Forgetting infrastructure | Manual hauling stays manual forever | Restore lifts and conveyors on sight |

## FAQ

**Is One Million Coins an idle game I can leave running?**

No. It is an active first-person incremental game. You collect coins by hand, carry them yourself, and deposit them manually. Automation exists, but it comes from restored bunker infrastructure like conveyors and cargo lifts, not from closing the game and waiting.

**How long does it take to finish One Million Coins?**

The developer states that most players should be able to work through the vault in around two hours. That figure assumes you keep upgrading steadily and do not stall on an inefficient build.

**What should I upgrade first when learning One Million Coins how to play?**

Capacity. Pouches and backpacks raise how many coins you can carry per trip, which is the earliest and hardest bottleneck. Collection speed feels good but does not help if you are constantly walking back to deposit.

**Do I need to fill the entire skill tree?**

No — and you probably will not. The tree has more than 250 nodes across seven paths, which is far more than a two-hour run can complete. Pick a direction, commit to it, and let the unused nodes be replay value for a second playthrough.
