**One Million Coins** is a short, first-person incremental game about scooping gold out of an underground vault with your bare hands and slowly turning yourself into an industrial mining machine. If you want a One Million Coins beginner guide that skips the fluff, this is it — the whole game is built to be finished in roughly two hours, so your first few decisions compound quickly. A One Million Coins beginner guide matters here because the opening 20 minutes set the pace for the entire run.

There is no combat, no timer, and no fail state. The only enemy is travel time. Every upgrade you buy either shortens the walk between the coin pile and the deposit station or increases how much gold you can carry on that walk. Once you internalize that, the game becomes a straightforward optimization puzzle — and a satisfying one.

## The Core Loop and How to Start Strong

The game hands you an empty pocket and exactly one million coins. You cannot buy anything until you bank something, so the loop is always the same four beats.

| Loop stage | What you do | What it unlocks |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Collect | Scoop coins from the pile by hand or with tools | Loose gold in your bag |
| Carry | Walk your haul to a deposit station | The chance to convert |
| Bank | Turn carried coins into spendable funds | Your upgrade budget |
| Upgrade | Spend funds on tools, bags, or skill nodes | A faster, bigger next haul |
| Expand | Push deeper and restore bunker infrastructure | Shorter trips, faster deposits |

Notice that "Expand" is the step most beginners skip. Restoring infrastructure does not feel like progress because it does not increase your coin count — but it is often the single biggest speed gain available at that moment.

### Your First 20 Minutes, Step by Step

| Step | Rough timing | Action | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | First 60 seconds | Scoop with bare hands until you can afford your first upgrade | Hands are free; you have nothing to lose |
| 2 | 1–5 min | Bank at the *closest* deposit station, not the farthest | Travel is your real cost early on |
| 3 | 5–10 min | Buy a pouch, then a backpack | Capacity multiplies every future trip |
| 4 | 10–15 min | Put your first nodes into collection speed and movement | Faster loops fund everything else |
| 5 | 15–20 min | Restore the first deposit station you find | Turns a long walk into a short one |

If you follow only one piece of advice from this One Million Coins beginner guide, make it step 2. New players instinctively walk to the big, obvious deposit point and burn 30 to 40 seconds per trip doing it. Those seconds add up to minutes, and minutes are the only currency the game actually charges you.

## Skill Tree Priorities in This One Million Coins Beginner Guide

The skill tree is the heart of the game: more than 250 nodes spread across seven upgrade paths. The store page names six of the affected stats — movement, reach, collection speed, tool strength, capacity, and deposit rate — and the seventh path supports broader scaling.

| Upgrade path | What it improves | Beginner priority |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Collection speed | How fast you scoop coins from the pile | High |
| Capacity | How much you can carry per trip | High |
| Movement | How quickly you cross the vault | High |
| Reach | How far you can grab coins from | Medium |
| Tool strength | How much each tool pulls per cycle | Medium |
| Deposit rate | How fast banked coins convert | Medium |
| Support / scaling | Late-game multipliers and utilities | Low early, high late |

### How to Spend Nodes Without Regret

- **Specialize in 20-minute blocks.** Pour nodes into one stat until the improvement is obvious, then pivot.
- **Never split evenly.** A flat spread across seven paths leaves every stat mediocre, and nothing feels better.
- **Revisit capacity often.** It is the quiet multiplier behind every other upgrade.
- **Treat deposit rate as a late-game stat.** Early on, your bottleneck is walking, not banking.

Community reports from the game's early buyers suggest that players who front-load movement and capacity finish noticeably faster than those who chase raw tool power first. That tracks with the math: a stronger tool does nothing if your pockets are full and you are standing 40 seconds from the deposit station.

## Tools, Bags, and Carry Capacity

Progression runs from bare hands to high-powered late-game equipment that pulls in waves of coins rather than pinches. Between those extremes sits a ladder of gear that changes how the game feels.

| Tier | Typical gear | What it feels like |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Bare hands | Slow, tiny loads, constant trips |
| 2 | Pouches | Your first real capacity boost |
| 3 | Backpacks | Long trips finally worth making |
| 4 | Powered collection tools | Coins arrive in scoops, not pinches |
| 5 | Late-game high-powered tools | Waves of coins per pass |

The temptation is to buy the flashiest tool the moment you can afford it. Resist that for the first half hour. A bigger bag with a modest tool beats a powerful tool with a small bag almost every time, because the bag pays out on *every* trip while the tool only pays out while you are scooping.

A practical buy order for beginners:

1. Pouch
2. Backpack
3. One mid-tier tool
4. Second capacity upgrade
5. Tool strength nodes
6. High-powered late-game gear

## Restoring the Bunker: Infrastructure and Digging Deeper

The vault is not just a pile of coins — it is an underground facility with deposit stations, cargo lifts, conveyors, and other bunker infrastructure, all of which start in disrepair. Restoring them is how you shrink the map.

- **Deposit stations** cut travel time by giving you closer places to bank.
- **Cargo lifts** move you and your haul vertically without the long climb.
- **Conveyors** keep coins flowing while you focus on the pile.
- **Deeper excavation** opens fresh sections of the bunker and, eventually, the last pockets of gold.

Think of infrastructure as permanent upgrades to your *route* rather than your character. A restored lift can save you more time per hour than three skill nodes combined, and it costs a single one-time payment.

## Beginner Mistakes and a Realistic Two-Hour Route

| Mistake | Why it hurts | Fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Banking at the far station | Wastes 30+ seconds per trip | Bank close, restore the rest later |
| Buying tools before bags | Powerful scoop, tiny pockets | Capacity first, power second |
| Spreading skill nodes evenly | Nothing gets noticeably better | Specialize, then pivot |
| Ignoring infrastructure | You walk the same long path forever | Restore deposits, lifts, conveyors |
| Hoarding coins | Unspent gold earns you nothing | Bank every single haul |
| Racing to the bottom | You leave coins behind and re-walk | Clear a section fully, then descend |

## FAQ

**How long does One Million Coins take to finish?**
The developer's own estimate is roughly two hours for most players. Community reports from early players generally land in that range, though completionists who sweep every corner of the bunker may take longer.

**Should I buy tools or capacity first in One Million Coins?**
Capacity. Bags and pouches pay off on every trip, while tool power only pays off while you are scooping. This One Million Coins beginner guide recommends a pouch and a backpack before your first serious tool purchase.

**Do I need to restore bunker infrastructure to finish?**
You can technically ignore it, but you will spend far more time walking. Deposit stations, cargo lifts, and conveyors shorten your route permanently, which is the fastest route to clearing all one million coins.

**Can I play at my own pace?**
Yes. The game is single-player, supports saving anytime, and the store page lists "playable at your own pace" among its accessibility features. There are no timers or fail states pushing you forward.

If you take one thing away from this guide, make it the loop itself: collect, bank, upgrade, and shorten the walk. One Million Coins rewards patience and small optimizations far more than frantic clicking, and two focused hours is all it takes to turn an empty pocket into an empty vault.
